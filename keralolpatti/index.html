About this translation

The Keralolpatti (“the origin of Kerala”) is the traditional Malayalam chronicle of how Kerala came to be: Parasurama raising the land from the sea and settling it with Brahmans, the age of the Perumals who were brought in to rule, and the age of the local rulers that followed, above all the rise of the Tamutiri (Zamorin) of Kozhikode. It is legend and tradition rather than history, compiled from older palm-leaf versions, and it is told from the point of view of the Brahman settlements and the Kozhikode court.

Hermann Gundert, the Basel Mission scholar, edited the Malayalam text and printed it at Mangalore in 1843. This translation follows the second edition (Stolz & Reuther, Basel Mission Press, Mangalore, 1868), which is in the public domain. The Malayalam was newly transcribed from the scan held at the Internet Archive (1868_Keralolpathi_Hermann_Gundert) and then translated.

How to read it

The translation is complete: every sentence, list and number of the original is here. The long chained sentences of the chronicle have been broken into ordinary English sentences and shorter paragraphs. Malayalam numerals are given as ordinary figures.

Numbers in grey square brackets, such as [12], mark where each page of the 1868 Malayalam edition begins, so a passage can be compared with the Malayalam text or the scan (printed page n is page n + 6 of the scan PDF).

Names are transliterated plainly, without diacritics, following the spelling of the text. Terms with no English equivalent (gramam, kazhakam, swarupam, nadu and so on) are kept and explained where they first appear.

Linked numbers in square brackets lead to notes, of three kinds. Unmarked notes are explanatory: they identify people, places, customs and terms, point out where the chronicle’s account differs from what historians hold, and flag inconsistencies within the text. They draw on general knowledge of Kerala history and are worded cautiously (“traditionally”, “probably”) where the matter is tradition or conjecture. Notes marked “Note in the 1868 edition” are the footnotes of the Malayalam edition itself. Notes marked “On the translation” say where the Malayalam is obscure or corrupt and the rendering is a best reading. A dotted underline marks a word that rests on an uncertain reading of the printed Malayalam.

The translation was then checked sentence by sentence against the Malayalam by a second reader, who also corrected the English.

The translation and the notes were made with machine assistance and have not been reviewed by a scholar of old Malayalam or of Kerala history. The chronicle is terse and in places corrupt, so some passages can be read in more than one way. Treat it as a reading translation, not as an authority.

Part 1. The Age of Parasurama

In the four ages called Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Kali,[1] many kings ruled and protected the earth in due order. After that, Sri Parasurama[2] became incarnate in order to destroy the wicked kings who had arisen in the Kshatriya race. Now of old, when Sri Parasurama had slain the crowned Kshatriyas twenty-one times over, he resolved that the sin of slaying heroes (virahatya) must be removed. To perform the rite for this he went to Gokarnam,[3] sat upon the rock hill, worshipped Varuna[4] and did penance, made the ocean withdraw, paid homage to the goddess Earth, and created 160 katam of land.[5] Resolving that the Malayalam country needed protection, he consecrated 108 images of the gods.

When he saw that even so the trembling of the earth had not ceased, Sri Parasurama took thought and provided Brahmans: he brought them from many quarters and settled them in Kerala. None of them stayed; they all went off to their own regions. [4] The reason was that serpents had come into Kerala and would not leave it, and because of their oppression no one was able to stay. After that the serpent lords (Nagas) held Kerala for some time. Resolving, “My labour must not prove fruitless,” Sri Parasurama went to the northern country and brought Arya Brahmans from Aryapuram. The Arya Brahmans first set out from the place called Ahichhatram[6] and stayed at the holy place called Samantapanchakam; that holy place also bears the name Kurukshetram.[7] From there Parasurama made all 64 gramams (Brahman settlements) set out, and brought them here. He considered the usage of the foreign country (paradesam),[8] where each separate agraharam[9] is called a gramam, and decided that it should not hold here; he ordained 64 gramams and gave names to all 64.

They are these:[10] Gokarnam, Gomakudam, Karavalli, Mallur, Eppanur, Cheppanur, Kadalur, Kallannur, Karyachchira, Paiyanchira, these ten gramams; Trikkani, Trikkatta, Trikkanpala, Trichchola, Kollur, Komalam, Vellara, Vengadu, Venkadam, Chengodu, these ten gramams; Kodiswaram, Manchiswaram, Uduppu, Sankaranarayanam, Kottam, Sivavelli, Mora, Pancha, Vittal, Kumaramangalam, Anantapuram, Kannapuram, these 12 gramams. Thus he ordained 32 gramams.

Paiyanur, Perinchellur, Karikkadu, Isanamangalam, Alattur, Karintolam, Trissivaperur, Panniyur, Chovaram, Sivapuram, these [5] ten; Parappur, Airanikkulam, Mushikakkulam, Iringadikkodu, Adappur, Chenganodu, Uliyanur, Kazhutanadu, Kazhachchur, Ilibhyam, Chamunda, Avadiputtur, these twelve; Kadukaruka, Kidangur, Karanallur, Kaviyur, Ettulaniyur, Nilmanna, Anmani, Anmalam, Tiruvallayi, Chenganiyur. Thus he ordained 64 gramams.[11]

He placed them at Gokarnam, had their heads shaved, and made them wear the tuft of hair in front. “The front tuft (purvasikha) is forbidden in the foreign country; if they wear the tuft in front, then, should they go back there, their own caste-fellows will not receive them”: it was for this reason that he had the tuft placed in front.[12] After that he gave the land to all sixty-four together, with flower and water,[13] saying, “Enjoy it as brahmakshatram.”[14] That gift was ekodakam (a single joint gift by the pouring of water).

After that, deciding that the land must be protected, Sri Parasurama said to those of the 64: “You will need the practice of arms; for that, receive weapons from me.” They all took counsel together and concluded: “If we take up weapons we shall become of the royal portion (rajamsam) and lose our part in penance; we shall be unfit to recite the Veda; our standing as Brahmans and all our many rites will be impaired.” Having so concluded, out of the 64 they took Perinchellur 3,000, Paiyanur 2,000, Panniyur 4,000, Parappur 5,000, Chenganniyur[15] 5,000, Alattur 1,000, [6] Uliyanur 5,000, Chenganodu 5,000, Airanikkulam 4,000, Mushikakkulam 1,000, Kazhutanadu 1,000; thus in ten and a half gramams[16] they set apart certain men of 14 gotrams[17] and appointed 36,000 Brahmans.

The 36,000 Brahmans went together, acting in the place of the 64 gramams, and with their consent received weapons from Sri Parasurama; and he taught them the use of weapons.[18] Saying, “Rule and protect the land of Kerala, 160 katam from Kanyakumari[19] to Gokarnam,” he poured water upon the sword and gave it. They stretched out their hands three times and received the water. Those of the Bharadvaja gotram received the weapon from Sri Parasurama with the words “Grant us the alms of the weapon (sastrabhiksha)”; with the consent of all they held out their hands and took it. Because they received sword and land by the command of Sri Parasurama, he gave them the name Vazhavar (rulers). To put anyone to death they need obtain no one's consent.

(Those who first received weapons: 1. Idappalli Nambiyatiri;[20] then 2. Venganattu Nambiyatiri, 3. Kanittalappandala, 4. Putumanakkattu Nambiyatiri, 5. Ilampayilindala, 6. Punnattur Nambidi, 7. Talayur Mussad, 8. Pilantoli Mussad, 9. Chozhatta Elayathu, 10. Kuzhimannu Mussad, 11. Kallukkatta Elayathu, 12. Ponninilattu Mumpil. These twelve men were appointed as the foremost.) Some have supposed that the [7] reason for the title “Tangal” is the power of penance. That is not so. Because their gotram received the alms of the weapon and they became “Val Nambi” (Nambi of the sword), the settled explanation is that the sword is in their own (tangalude) hands.[21] In this way 36,000 Brahmans were appointed bearers of arms to protect the land.

Then he summoned all 64 gramams together and said: “To the serpents that tormented you in former days a share has been given everywhere out of the Brahman property (brahmaswam). They are to be your local deities and tutelary deities and to protect you.” So ordaining, he gave them each a share out of the brahmaswam and won their favour, and he commanded, “Perform offerings and rites of worship to them, and maintain them.” He set them up as local deities, and the plague of serpents left Kerala.[22]

After that he made in Kerala, for the bearers of arms, 1,008 fencing grounds of forty-two feet (nalpattiradi),[23] appointed many tutelary deities of the kalari (fencing school), and had lamps and worship offered there. He installed the goddess Durga on the seashore and installed Sasta[24] beside the mountains; he installed Naga and Bhuta; he scattered gold dust upon the ground and pressed it in, established gold-water, had rasi coins[25] struck and laid up treasure. In this way he removed the trembling of the earth.

After that, hearing it said that the Arya Brahmans had settled firmly in Malayalam, the party that had formerly gone away for fear of the serpents [8] also came back. They all became the Old Tuluvar (Pazhantuluvar). In the Tulu country[26] they are called Tulu Nambis; they are not counted among the 64.

After that Sri Parasurama summoned all 64 gramams, brought them to Vellappanadu and settled them there. He built 64 maths (lodges) for the 64 gramams, and marked off and assigned 64 desams (localities). To each gramam he allotted a separate desam and property for its enjoyment. No gramam was left without property and a family house (taravadu) in Vellappanadu. Everyone has a place there; he ordained that for the 64 gramams Vellappanadu should be the chief seat.

To some of Perumana gramam he assigned the office of expounding the Puranas. To some of the party that came the second time he gave the office of tantra;[27] the office of tantra was not assigned to the 64 gramams as a whole. Among the 64 gramams, the office of the vattakam (ritual vessel), beginning with the taking up of the hand-vessel (kaivattaka), has been assigned to four or six gramams, Iringadikudu and Tarananallur among them.[28] To Payyanur gramam he assigned the Nambi's share (nambi-kuru) everywhere.

Then he said to those of the 64: “If hereafter deities come into Kerala and torment men, and affliction by deities increases, people will suffer untimely death; that must not be.” So he appointed 12 men in six gramams of the 64, and gave the 12 men instruction in mantras.[29] They are these. First, in Perunchellur gramam [9] he appointed two, “Adikachcheri” and “Kalakattu”. He commanded that, to ward off the evil deities that come from the mountains, the evil mantras (durmantram) should be practised and the evil deities held back, and that in time of calamity Bhadra[30] should be worshipped and the calamities removed. Seeing that this impaired the rites of the Brahmans, he gave a second command, to Kalakkattu: to hold back the water deities that come from the seashore he should practise the good mantras (sanmantram) and win the favour of the deity of good rites; and if in time of calamity he worshipped Durga, the calamity would pass.

Next, in Karikattu gramam he assigned both the evil mantras and the good mantras to the two, “Kaniyoda” and “Kattumadam”. Next, in Alattur gramam he commanded the two, “Kakkadu” and “Kuzhimana”, to prevail by the evil mantras and by the good mantras. Next he appointed Putukkodu and Putumana in Chovaram, the pair “Kallukadu” and “Kakkattukolam” in Perumana gramam, and the two “Chundakkadu” and “Muttemana” in Iringadikkude gramam. Thus he set six men to serve the deity of the evil mantras, so as to hold back the evil deities that come from the mountains, and six men to serve the deity of the good mantras, so as to hold back the deities that come from the sea; and in this way he appointed twelve men in Kerala, of the highest and the middle kind, as sampradayis (hereditary practitioners).

After that Sri Parasurama [10] said, “Who will take upon himself my sin of slaying heroes?” Hearing this, certain men of the Bharadvaja gotram took the sin of slaying heroes upon themselves. They are the people of Ravananattukare gramam; he gave them the name “the party of Iril”,[31] and said: “You must have one god as your chief. For that, worship Subrahmanya;[32] then the sorrows and great diseases that befall you will pass away, and your prosperity and your line will greatly increase. Serve especially those who are Nambis of the sword.” So saying, he gave them much property.

When he commanded those of the 64, “In this Kerala all must follow descent through the mother (matriparamparyam); my own love too is for the mother,”[33] all were sorely troubled in mind. Then the people of Paiyanur gramam took counsel, resolved that they must obey as Parasurama had commanded, and did adopt descent through the mother. He commanded that the people of certain other gramams should follow it also; after that, no one obeyed. Then he brought Sudras of many kinds from the foreign country and made them duly follow descent through the mother; and he ordained that they should serve as escort to the 64 gramams, and that their protection should be the Brahmans themselves.[34]

Thus Sri Parasurama created Malayalam, the land of rites (karmabhumi), and made the gift by water [11] to the Brahmans in 64 gramams. What was first given to all 64 gramams jointly, with flower and water, to be enjoyed, is called janmam.[35] That gift was ekodakam, given jointly to the taravadu-holders in each gramam. What was afterwards given, by pouring water upon the sword, to the 36,000 Brahmans of 14 gotrams in the ten and a half gramams[36] is the royal portion. These may dip the finger in the water saying “my janmam”; the others must not dip the finger saying “my janmam”, for they may dip only for enjoyment (anubhavam). When they dip among themselves they must dip the finger saying “enjoyment for me”. If anyone, not knowing this, dips his finger for janmam, the finger will not come straight again.[37] What was given to those of the 36,000 was not ekodakam; he appointed them bearers of arms to protect the land.

Desiring that the people living in this Kerala should be like the dwellers in heaven, that the land should be equal to the world of the gods, and that they should enjoy the bliss of heaven, Sri Parasurama gave the charge of it to Devendra[38] and went away to do penance. There must be six months of rain; many and many kinds of crops must grow in the kingdom; food, flowers, water and grass must be had in due measure; gifts and charity must increase and there must be prosperity; there being prosperity, the service of the gods must be duly performed; the worship of the gods and the worship of the ancestors must be performed; for that there must be many cows, and for them there must be grass [12] and water in plenty. For these reasons he gave the charge to Devendra, and that is why six months of the hot season are rainy.[39]

And having settled many gods in temples, in the groves of the deities, in Aiyappan groves, in the precincts of Bhadrakali, in Ganapati groves and elsewhere, he also ordained six months of summer sunshine, so that at the guarded shrines and sacred seats there might be feasting and song, and that festivals might be held such as these: utsavam, vela, vilakku, tiyattam, Bharani vela, arattu, kaliyattam, pura vela, daivattam, teyyattu, daivamattu, tannir-amritam, talappoli, Paiyavisakham, Mahamakham and the Mamangam vela.[40]

“In this land of rites created by Sri Parasurama, the gods of the earth (the Brahmans) are to bathe at dawn and live in purity, perform all the observances and rites that are proper to them, and, in order to avert the sorrows and great diseases that befall the rest of the people, perform the services of the gods that ought to be done: homam and meditation, the service of Bhagavati, pushpanjali (offering of flowers), antya-namaskaram, worship at the three times of the day, Ganapati-homam, Mrityunjayam, the Sahasranamam (the thousand names) three hundred thousand times, Dhanvantaram, graha-santi (appeasing of the planets), sahasra-bhojanam (the feeding of a thousand).[41] By performing many such services of the gods they are to increase merit.” So Sri Parasurama commanded the Veda Brahmans, and the Veda Brahmans accepted it, saying, “So be it.”

While things stood thus, Brahmans of many parties, hearing it said that the people living in Kerala [13] were equal to the dwellers in heaven, came into Kerala from many quarters. Sri Parasurama then settled them in many places, and assigned them many stations in many desams.

The Veda Brahmans had the gift of land received through the half-Brahmans,[42] and made it into separate desams in their names. In each desam they built a temple and performed the consecration, performed worship and the sivaveli offering before the image, and adorned it with full garlands. They settled in the gramam their own local deity and the tutelary deities of the place, and settled the deity of the village shrine. They had the vela, the vilakku, the feast and the tira that were due in each place provided for. They brought Sudras from many quarters and settled them, and laid down many customs for them. They established serfdom and tenancy in the desam and protected the serfs and the tenants. They fixed the tara[43] and the sanketam,[44] appointed Nayars within the tara, and through them ordained “the eye, the hand and the command” (oversight, execution and order). They took care that rights should suffer neither decline nor lapse. They ordained the lower right (kizhayma) for the tenants and the higher right (melayma) and overlordship (melazhi) for themselves; kanam for the tenants and janmam for themselves;[45] and they carried on the custom of kanam and janmam. They laid down the Brahman observances and the Sudra customs, had the illams (houses) of the Brahmans of the gramams built in the villages, ordained their own worship of the gods and of the ancestors, maintained truth and justice, gladdened [14] the Veda Brahmans of the 64 gramams, and gave gifts and charity. So matters stood.

Those of the 36,000, the half-Brahmans, by receiving the gift of land and by taking upon themselves the sin of slaying heroes, lost half of their Brahmanhood. The half-Brahmans, as bearers of arms, keep guard, take the field and serve as escort; therefore they became Val Nambi. The Pattini Nambi (the fasting Nambi) has no weapon other than the conch and the umbrella. If he has a grievance, he goes to the bathing-place of the tank, and whether men mock him or kill him he will only blow his conch and sit there fasting (pattini); and he also keeps a Val Nambi standing near him.[46]

Resolving that the Brahmans must not hereafter dispute among themselves, each claiming his share and his rank, and so bring defect upon the rites and cause the land of rites to decay, he gathered into one place the 64, those of the 36,000 beginning with the three thousand from Perinchellur, and the Brahmans of many parties who had come from many quarters, and said to them: “When a little time has gone by you will quarrel with one another; that must not be.” So he appointed four kazhakams (assemblies) to act in the place of the 64 gramams.[47] They are: first Perinchellur, then Paiyanur, then Parappur, then Chenganiyur.[48]

After those of the 36,000 had protected the kingdom for a long time, [15] they disputed among themselves, each claiming his share and his desam, and because punishment and protection were seen to fail in the land, all the Brahmans met and took counsel. They made the rule that it should be enough for these four kazhakams to meet in order to install, for three years at a time, one man from each of the four kazhakams as rakshapurushan (protector).[49] Know that because the four kazhakams lay far apart there was delay in business; so, to act in the place of the four kazhakams, four men were appointed in 4 desams of Perinchellur gramam itself.

Some say that of these four Chenganiyur does not belong to the 64 gramams. The people who say so have not understood the matter properly. The reason for the saying is this. Those of the Chenganiyur kazhakam, by a common resolution, gave a connection with their temple to all the 64. Certain Tamils came there and grew numerous. Those Tamils who had come and the Brahmans of the place fell out with one another over the burning of a corpse, and the Tamils were not able to perform the funeral rites. Thereupon the Tamils took counsel together, took with them the people of the place and those of the sixty-four to whom the connection with the temple had been given, dragged the corpse to the river and threw it in. For this reason some say that those of the Chenganiyur kazhakam are not counted with the 64. How they came to be Tamils, and how the guilt of Brahman-slaying fell upon them, God alone can know.

[16] Further, when one man in each of these four appointed kazhakams was to protect as rakshapurushan for three years at a time, a sixth share (shadbhagam)[50] was laid upon the property of everyone and given for the maintenance of the rakshapurushan and of those who went about with him. After a long time had passed in this way, those who were installed and held office from time to time came to be called Avarodha Nambi (the installed Nambi). The Avarodha Nambis are Kanjur, Kinongadu, Karingamvalli and many other such parties who hold property in the south and the north. Besides this, they have also acquired property in the south and the north on their own account.[51]

In the times when they were thus installed and held office, each thought, “My term is but three years; the thing to do in the meantime is to acquire property,” and they began to oppress the subjects in the land, swelled their wealth with bribes and hoarded treasure, and went to great lengths in this. When a little time had gone by in this way, the Brahmans considered: “All the property in the south and the north will pass to this installed party; that must not be.” Being of one mind, they resolved, “Let us appoint a king for each term.” If these installed Nambis should have to dip the finger declaring janmam for janmam, they have no title to janmam; if the others dip, they have only to go along with it.[52]

[The Brahmans met on the sandbank at Tirunavayi,[53] deliberated as an assembly, and resolved that henceforth one man from each of the ten and a half [17] gramams should govern the land for twelve years at a time. When they came to install men to take up the sword as rakshapurushans at Trikkariyur and Trikkottu, they disputed with one another, each saying “I” and “I”. After that they all took counsel together: “If the Brahmans govern the land henceforth, there will be neither punishment nor protection in the land. A king is now needed to govern the land.” Having so resolved, in order to make a king, and acting in the place of the 64 gramams, they made the rule that it should be enough for the four kazhakams of Panniyur, Parappur, Perinchellur and Chenganiyur to meet. The 4 kazhakams sat as one assembly and deliberated, set out and went to the foreign country, brought Keya Perumal (Perumal: emperor or viceroy) from Keyapuram, and installed him as ruler in the region called Kerala.]

Notes to Part 1

[1] The four yugas of Hindu cosmology, each shorter and more degenerate than the last. The Kali age, the present one, is traditionally reckoned from 3102 B.C. The chronicle does not keep the ages strictly apart: Parasurama belongs by tradition to the Treta age.

[2] “Rama with the axe”, son of the sage Jamadagni and, in the usual reckoning, the sixth incarnation of Vishnu. He is a Brahman warrior who, according to the Puranas, exterminated the Kshatriya (warrior and ruling) class twenty-one times over to avenge his father's murder. The whole west coast claims him as its creator.

[3] A place of pilgrimage on the coast of Karnataka, sacred to Siva. In this chronicle it is the northern limit of Parasurama's land, as Kanyakumari is the southern.

[4] The Vedic god of the waters and lord of the ocean. In the better-known form of the legend Parasurama throws his axe from Gokarnam and the sea withdraws as far as it falls.

[5] The katam is a traditional league, usually taken as about five miles, though its value varied from place to place. The 160 katam is the chronicle's fixed figure for the length of the land from Gokarnam to Kanyakumari, in fact some 500 miles.

[6] An ancient city of northern India, capital of the Panchala country in what is now Uttar Pradesh. Brahman communities in several parts of the Deccan and the west coast trace their origin to it.

[7] Kurukshetra, north of Delhi, is the battlefield of the Mahabharata. The lakes there, properly called Samantapanchaka, are said to have been filled by Parasurama with the blood of the slain Kshatriyas.

[8] Paradesam, “the other country”, is the chronicle's word throughout for the lands beyond the mountains to the east, chiefly the Tamil and Kannada country.

[9] A street or quarter of Brahman houses, usually a royal endowment, the normal form of Brahman settlement in the Tamil, Kannada and Telugu country. The Brahmans of Kerala lived instead in scattered houses, each attached to one of the gramams.

[10] The tradition of 64 original Brahman settlements, 32 in the Tulu country (coastal Karnataka) and 32 in Kerala proper, is common to the Brahmans of both regions. In the first list several names can be recognised, among them Gokarnam, Kollur, Manchiswaram (Manjeshwar), Uduppu (Udupi), Sankaranarayanam, Kottam (Kota), Sivavelli (Sivalli, beside Udupi) and Vittal.

[11] The 32 gramams of Kerala run from north to south. Those that can be placed with confidence include Paiyanur (Payyanur) and Perinchellur (Taliparamba) in the far north, Trissivaperur (Thrissur), Chovaram (Sukapuram) and Panniyur near the Bharatappuzha, Airanikkulam, Mushikakkulam (Muzhikkulam) and Iringadikkodu (Irinjalakuda) in the centre, and Kidangur, Kaviyur, Tiruvallayi (Tiruvalla), Anmalam (Aranmula) and Chenganiyur (Chengannur) in the south. The list is repeated, with a number of differences in names and spelling, in the section on the first Perumals below.

[12] The Nambutiri Brahmans of Kerala traditionally wore the tuft of hair (kudumi) knotted at the front of the crown, whereas Brahmans elsewhere in south India wear it at the back. The chronicle explains the difference as a device to stop the settlers from going home.

[13] In Hindu law a gift is made irrevocable by pouring water into the hand of the receiver; flowers accompany the water. Much of what follows turns on who received land by this rite and who did not.

[14] “Brahman dominion”: a land in which the Brahmans hold the power that elsewhere belongs to the Kshatriyas. The claim that all Kerala was given to the Brahmans in this way is the chronicle's central theme.

[15] The same gramam as the Chenganiyur of the list above; the 1868 text spells the name both ways.

[16] Eleven gramams are named, and their contingents do add up to 36,000. “Ten and a half” is the fixed traditional expression; presumably one of the eleven was counted as a half.

[17] A gotram is a Brahman clan reckoned in the male line from one of the Vedic sages, such as Bharadvaja, named below.

[18] These are the arms-bearing Brahmans, known later in the chronicle as Chattirar. Bodies of Brahmans trained to arms are a real feature of early medieval Kerala, where inscriptions mention them attached to the great temples.

[19] Cape Comorin, the southern tip of India, named after the virgin goddess (Kanya Kumari) whose temple stands there.

[20] The chief of Idappalli (Edappally, near Kochi), a Brahman who ruled a small territory of his own down to modern times. The titles in this list, Nambiyatiri, Nambidi, Mussad and Elayathu, belong to groups that rank a little below the Nambutiri Brahmans proper. The chronicle explains their lower standing by their having taken up arms.

[21] Tangal is an honorific plural, literally “themselves”, used as a title of respect. The chronicle plays on its literal sense.

[22] An origin legend for the serpent groves of Kerala. Until recent times almost every old house compound kept a patch of untouched woodland with stone images of the Nagas, where lamps were lit and offerings made.

[23] The kalari is the gymnasium and fencing school of Kerala, the home of the martial art now called kalarippayattu. The standard kalari is a sunken rectangular pit forty-two feet long, with a shrine to its guardian deities in one corner.

[24] Sasta, also called Aiyappan, is a god especially of Kerala, worshipped in forest and hill shrines, of which Sabarimala is now the most famous. Tradition accordingly sets a line of Durga temples along the coast and a line of Sasta temples along the mountains as guardians of the land.

[25] The rasi is the oldest known gold coin (panam or fanam) of Kerala. Popular belief ascribed its minting to Parasurama.

[26] The coastal country north of Kerala, around Mangalore and Udupi in present-day Karnataka, where the Tulu language is spoken.

[27] The tantri is the hereditary chief priest of a Kerala temple, who alone may consecrate the image and perform the highest rites. The office belongs to a small number of Brahman families.

[28] On the translation. Sense of this sentence uncertain. Iringadikudu is Irinjalakuda, and Tarananallur is the name of the family that holds the office of tantri there; the vattakam is apparently a vessel used in temple worship, standing here for a priestly office.

[29] This is the origin legend of the mantravadis, the Brahman families of Kerala who practise magic and exorcism as a hereditary profession. Some of the house names given here, such as Kalakattu and Kattumadam, are still borne by families of this kind.

[30] Bhadrakali, the fierce form of the goddess, as Durga, named just below, is her benign form.

[31] Probably the same as the Uril Parisha (“the party of the village”), a name borne by a section of the Mussads, who are traditionally said to have lost rank by accepting Parasurama's sin.

[32] The god of war, son of Siva, also called Skanda, Kartikeya or Murugan.

[33] According to the Puranas Parasurama cut off the head of his mother, Renuka, at his father's command, and then obtained her restoration to life as a boon. Kerala tradition makes the matrilineal law his atonement.

[34] The Sudras of the chronicle are chiefly the Nayars, the military and landholding caste of Kerala, among whom family property and membership descended in the female line (marumakkattayam). The Nambutiris were patrilineal, with the single exception of those of Payyanur, who did in fact follow the matrilineal rule.

[35] Janmam, literally “birth”, is the hereditary and absolute proprietary right in land, the highest title known to Kerala custom. It was held mostly by Brahmans, temples and ruling families, and every lesser tenure was derived from it.

[36] On the translation. The text reads aru pattu, “six ten”; the ten and a half gramams named above seem to be meant.

[37] Transfers of land were solemnised with water, and dipping the finger seems to be the gesture by which a party declared the right he was conveying or claiming. The passage is not wholly consistent with what was said above, where it was the 64 gramams that received the land as a joint gift.

[38] Indra, king of the gods, who sends the rain.

[39] Kerala receives both monsoons: the south-west from June to September and the north-east in October and November. The festival season falls in the dry months that follow.

[40] A list of temple and shrine festivals. Utsavam is the annual temple festival, which ends with the arattu, the ceremonial bathing of the image. Vela, vilakku (“lamp”) and puram are festivals of the goddess shrines; tiyattam, kaliyattam and teyyattu are ritual dances in which a performer impersonates the deity; talappoli is a procession of women carrying lamps on platters. Mahamakham or Mamangam is the great twelve-yearly festival at Tirunavayi, of which the chronicle has much to say later.

[41] Homam is an offering made into the sacred fire. Bhagavati is the goddess; Ganapati (Ganesa) is the remover of obstacles; Mrityunjayam is a rite addressed to Siva as conqueror of death, and Dhanvantaram one addressed to Dhanvantari, the divine physician, both performed for health and long life.

[42] To accept a gift, and above all a gift of land from one who has shed blood, transfers the giver's sin to the receiver. The Brahmans who kept to the Veda are therefore said to have let the arms-bearing “half-Brahmans” take the gift on their behalf.

[43] The tara was the unit of Nayar local organisation, a neighbourhood whose heads of families met to manage their common affairs.

[44] A sanketam was the territory of a temple or of a Brahman settlement, governed by its own council and treated as a sanctuary exempt from the authority of the local ruler.

[45] Kanam was the commonest tenure of Malabar: the tenant advanced a sum of money to the janmam proprietor and in return held the land, paying a rent, for a term that was customarily renewed. Here the whole system of landlord and tenant is traced to the Brahmans' original title.

[46] Fasting in public against an opponent was the recognised weapon of a Brahman: if he died, the guilt of Brahman-slaying fell on the person who had wronged him. Pattini is the ordinary Malayalam word for going without food.

[47] A kazhakam is a council or assembly. The four kazhakams are the four leading gramams, whose joint meeting was held to speak for all the Brahmans of Kerala.

[48] When the four kazhakams are named again below, Panniyur stands in the place of Paiyanur. Panniyur, Perinchellur, Parappur and Chenganiyur is the usual form of the tradition.

[49] “Protecting man”: a Brahman elected to hold executive power for a fixed term. The chronicle describes three stages of government: the armed Brahmans as a body, then elected protectors, and finally kings brought in from outside.

[50] One sixth of the produce is the share that the Sanskrit law-books allow a king to take from his subjects in return for protecting them.

[51] On the translation. Literally “have made it with their own”; the last word may mean their own means or their own kinsmen.

[52] On the translation. Last sentence obscure in the original. It seems to deny the installed Nambis any power to convey land in full ownership.

[53] Tirunavayi (Tirunavaya) stands on the north bank of the Bharatappuzha, the river the chronicle calls the Perar, in the present Malappuram district. Part 2. The Age of the Perumals

1. The first Perumals

Then, in order to make a king, they all went together to the foreign country and brought back a Kshatriya and a Kshatriya woman. They agreed together by covenant that Brahmans should marry the Kshatriya woman and that the offspring born of this should be Kshatriyas.[1] There is something more that could be told [18] about this at length, but it is not needed here. The Kshatriya who was brought at that time bears the name Cheraman-Keralan Perumal.[2] This is the king of Malanadu (the hill country). The king of Chozhamandalam is called Chozha Perumal, and the king of Pandimandalam is called Pandi Perumal and also Kulasekhara Perumal.[3] Such are the Perumals.

Malanadu is made up of 4 divisions. From Gokarnam to Perumpuzha in the Tulu country is the Tulu kingdom. From Perumpuzha[4] to Putupattanam is the Kuva kingdom.[5] From Putupattanam to Kannetti is the Kerala kingdom. From Kannetti to Kanyakumari is the Mushika kingdom. These are the names of the 4 divisions.[6] In Kerala there are 11 anacharams; in the foreign country there are 22 anacharams.[7]

When the king was first brought and set up, the Brahmans clasped hands with him and made a covenant in this manner: “What we are not able to accomplish, you are to accomplish, and so keep us protected. If we are wronged, or if dangers arise, and we then transact the affairs of the kingdom ourselves, the king may only say, ‘What is this? After appointing me, you transact the business yourselves.’ There is to be no questioning of the Brahmans.” Therefore even today, when any offences occur, the king can only say, “You are dealing with it yourselves; why did you not [19] bring your complaint to us?” That comes of the original covenant. In other kingdoms one need not go looking for the king.[8] In Kerala it was with all this reserved that everyone gave property for the king to enjoy.

The Brahmans of the 14 gotrams from Ahichhatram met, poured out rice (or: poured out water), and gave him a good vritti (a holding for his maintenance); to this day it is called virutti, that is, vritti.[9] As for the royal revenue (rajabhogam), in some places it was given; in some places the Brahmans decided that it belonged to themselves; in some places the temple is chief and the king has a right of enjoyment. They also gave the king a seat to reign from, and appointed for it Allur, the Perunkovilakam (great palace) of Kodungallur.[10]

It was only after Keya Perumal and the Brahmans had clasped each other's hands and made many covenants and oaths that he was appointed to rule Malanadu. Then they assigned to that Perumal royal revenue and virutti land in Malanadu. For the Perumal to reside in they built a kovilakam (palace) at the place called Talayur, north of Talipparamba.[11] Resolving that Kerala, the land of Parasurama, must be duly governed, they took Keya Perumal by the hand and seated him to rule for twelve years, in the Kali year given by the chronogram “bhumau bhupoyam prapya” (“this king having come to the earth”),[12] Kali 4941, A.D. 216.[13]

That Perumal governed the land for 8 years and 4 months, and then ascended to heaven. When the twelve years [20] were over, the ceremony agreed between that Perumal and the Brahmans was held.[14] When the reign of Keya Perumal had thus ended and he had gone to heaven, they brought Chozha Perumal from Chozhamandalam, appointed him to rule and govern Kerala for 12 years and performed the installation, and built a kovilakam called Chozhakkara for the Perumal to reside in. After reigning 10 years and 2 months he went back to Chozhamandalam.

After that they brought Pandi Perumal from Pandimandalam, took him by the hand and seated him at the place called Pandivampana, and performed the installation. This Perumal built a fort there. After he had ruled the land for 9 years, men came from Pandimandalam saying, “There is no one to protect Pandimandalam,” and that Perumal went back to Pandimandalam.

Formerly a certain Bhutaraya Pandya Perumal ruled Kerala. He had two bhutas (spirits) to guard his person and do him service. While this Perumal was carrying on the government, the hatred of the Brahmans against him grew very strong, and they determined that he must be killed by one means or another. When they tried sorcery, they saw that so long as he had the help of these bhutas [21] it would be hard indeed to kill that Perumal, and they resolved that some trick must be used to get the bhutas away. One Bhattatiri[15] swore, “I will go, get the bhutas out of the way, kill him and come back,” and set out. He went to the Perumal, played chess with him,[16] defeated the Perumal, and began to win stake after stake. In this way he at length won both these bhutas as slaves bound to do his own service; and he sent them off, saying, “Go and see how many waves come in upon the sea, and bring me the count.” The bhutas went to the seashore and counted the waves, and, never coming to the end of them, remained there and did not return.

He then sent word to the other Brahmans that the Perumal should be killed that evening. The Brahmans of all 10 gramams came to the kovilakam fully armed, and this Bhattatiri then slew him. Afterwards, reflecting, “There is the sin of having done violence,” he said, “I will sit upon the step (padi),” and sat down on a separate step. From that day his name became Nambidi.[17] He is the Nambidi known as Kakkattu Karanappadu.

How the name Bhutarayar arose is told in the Kerala-mahatmyam,[18] chapter 90: “Pandya-[22] bhupas samagatya senabhir bhuta-sankule” (“The Pandya king, coming with his armies amid a throng of bhutas”), and so on. When that Pandyan had invaded Malayalam with armies of bhutas and was building a temple called Bhutanathan and a bazaar, Parasurama said to him in anger, “Yushmakam chetu mad-bhumav evam agamanam vritha” (“Your coming thus into my land is in vain”), and “Adityaya maya datta”, that is, “I have given it to the southern king named Adityavarman.” After that there was a battle; the bhutas were defeated, and the place called Bhutapandi became the boundary of the country.[19]

From the beginning of the Kali age wicked men came and committed outrages, and those of the 64 gramams therefore petitioned Sri Parasurama that a king should be appointed for each term. He said: “At the holy Nava temple, on the day when the goddess Ganga comes for the festival of the Lord of Tirunavayi,[20] bathe; make king the man who pleases you, without giving him the sixth share of the land; and anoint him with the water of the Perar.” Then, for the destruction of enemies and the protection of the temples, Parasurama obtained the sword of Bhadrakali and gave it into the hands of the Brahmans. They all went together to the kingdom called Chozhamandalam, and the one named Keralan …[21] The account told in the Tulu country is that a rich man named Bhutala Pandi, with the help of bhutas, came to the Tulu country by ship, and after becoming king at Perakkur took to wife [23] 12 maidens from among the Jains, divided the Tulu kingdom among their children, and ordained the anacharam called marumakkattayam (inheritance through sisters' sons).[22]

They brought the king, and when Jupiter stood in Cancer, in the month of Kumbham, which is Magha, on the day of the asterism Puyam,[23] they bathed in the Perar, and stayed at the Nava temple on the bank of the Perar, a holy river whose sacred water flows out of the sacrificial fire-pit of the great sage Agastya[24] and joins the sea. They seated him upon the hall of state at Pakayur and anointed him king of the land that Sri Parasurama had given.

They gave him ankam (fees on duels), chunkam (tolls), vazhipizha (fines for offences), ampavari, aimmula-mummula (cows with five teats or three), chenkompu-kadakan, pullinarival, kinattil-panni (a wild pig fallen into a well), attu-tiruttu (land thrown up by a river), land left by the sea, talappu and the sea tolls; and of whatever is produced in this Kerala, silam and mulam, all things whatsoever of these kinds;[25][26] and they gave him the sword of Bhadrakali, who had graciously stood witness at the temple, which Parasurama had put into their hands. They had military service done by their own servants.

He made his capital at Trikkadamatilakam. Residing there he duly protected Kerala for 12 years and then went away to his own kingdom. Because of the surpassing virtues of that king the name Kerala arose.[27]

Then the Brahmans went to the Pandi kingdom and brought the king called Pandiyan, who was a Chengar,[28] and anointed him as before. When that king had protected the land for 12 [24] years, they made him render his account and lay down the sword, and conducted the king back to the Pandi kingdom. They went to Chozhamandalam and brought the king named Chozhiyan; that king protected Kerala for 12 years. Then came the Perumal named Kulasekharan, from the Pandya kingdom.[29]

After a king had thus been appointed to protect Malanadu, they considered that the king, when a little time had passed, would encroach. So that this should not happen, they surveyed the 160 katam of Kerala and made the 160 katam into 17 nadus (districts), and they decreed that the affairs of the kingdom should therefore be settled only in joint deliberation, and that the king must not act by himself. They carried on the daily business together with the king, and close beside the kovilakam they built 4 talis for the parties appointed for the 4 kazhakams to sit in: Mettali, Kizhttali, Nediyattali and Chingapurattali.[30] Those who sit in these talis and protect the land are called Taliyatiris. Kizhttali is for Airanikkudam, Chingapuram for Idingadikkudam, Nediyattali for Paravur, Melttali for Mushikakkulam; these are the 4 talis.

Because Panniyur, Perinchellur and Chenganiyur all lay far apart from one another, Airanikkudam, Mushikakkulam and Iringanikkudam, which are near Paravur, came together with Paravur to bear the name of the 4 kazhakams.[31] These four were appointed in the time when the Perumals protected the land; the other kazhakams arose in the time [25] of Parasurama. In the time of the Taliyatiris, whenever a writ (tittu) had to be written, it had always to be written as a “Taliyatiri-tittu”.

Those that entered upon the office of Taliyatiri and so came into being are the Karingampulli swarupam (ruling house), the Karyamukkil swarupam and the Karimukka Ilampara Kotta swarupam.[32] The reason why the junior members of these swarupams became Taliyatiris is this. In Malanadu the sixth share was not given to the king; only a vritti was given him. The sixth share upon everyone's property was enjoyed by the rakshapurushans, and after them by the Taliyatiris. Later it was assigned to the Chattirar, and so the Chattirar have it to this day.[33][34]

After the king and the Taliyatiris had thus protected the land together for a little while, the 32 gramams north of the Payaswini Perumpuzha and the 32 gramams south of it broke off intermarriage and dealings with each other. The 32 of the south are these. North of the Karuman river, 10 gramams. The particulars: 1. Payyanur, 2. Perinchellur, 3. Karikkattu, 4. Isanimangalam, 5. Alattur, 6. Karintolam,[35] 7. Trissivaperur (Trichchamperur), 8. Perumanam, 9. Panniyur, 10. Chovvaram. South of the Karuman river and north of the Punyar, 12 gramams. They are: 1. Paravur, 2. Airanikkulam, 3. Mushikakkulam, 4. Iringanikkudam, 5. Adavur, 6. Chenganadu, 7. Uliyanur, 8. Kazhutunadu, 9. [26] Kuzhayur, 10. Ilibhyam, 11. Chamunda, 12. Avattipputtur; thus 12 gramams. South of the Punyar and north of Kanyakumari, 10 gramams: 1. Kidangur, 2. Kadukaruka, 3. Karanellur, 4. Kaviyur, 5. Ettumanur, 6. Nirmannu, 7. Anmani, 8. Anmalam (Ammalam, Mangalam), 9. Chenganiyur, 10. Tiruvilapayi; thus 10 gramams. In all 32.[36]

As to the remaining 32 gramams: those called Old Tuluvar and Tulu Nambis, who had gone away among the Pancha-Dravidas[37] and come back, and those who had joined themselves to them, exerted themselves, each saying, “I, I belong to the thirty-two.” They practised the customs of the foreign country and began to intermarry with its people; they attached themselves to the kings of the foreign country, whose koyma (authority) came to prevail;[38] they were constituted as separate gramams, and each was given a name brought from one or another of many gramams. That is how they came to be called gramams. There are many different accounts; the truth is as told here.[39]

2. The Buddhist Perumal

Then, when the Kali age had advanced a little, the Brahmans went to the foreign country and brought Bana Perumal from Banapuram. They took him by the hand and seated him in the Perunkoyilakam of Allur. While that Perumal was reigning, the Bauddhas (Buddhists)[40] came and saw the Perumal. When they had made him hear the whole authority of the Bauddha scripture, the Perumal was persuaded that “this indeed [27] is the truth”, and the Perumal of that day joined the Bauddha way. That Perumal summoned the Brahmans and began to question them, and he commanded that everyone in this Malanadu must follow this way. At this they were all at their wits' end and withdrew to Trikkariyur.[41]

While they were living together at Trikkariyur, and the leading men of the gramams had to maintain them all, they got their daily living by serving many kinds of people, and so were not able to keep the rules of purity and impurity. When their distress of mind had become very great, by the grace of God a great sage came to that place; his name was Jangaman. All the Brahmans of the place went to that sage together and laid their trouble before him. The sage said: “I will teach you an expiation to remove the sin of impurity that arises in this place where food is cooked and served. It is this: after sunset let the Brahmans set up a lamp and walk round the lamp in reverence (dipa-pradakshinam).” For performing the dipa-pradakshinam the sage also taught them a song; this song is a hymn in praise of Brahma. He said further: “As you must have one god as your chief to sing it to, the Lord of Trikkariyur himself is your tutelary deity. If you sing this every day, a way will open for all your troubles [28] to pass away.” Having said this, the sage departed.[42]

Then, when the Brahmans had set up a lamp after sunset and begun to walk round the lamp, six Sastris[43] came from the foreign country: one was Bhattacharyan, one Bhatta Banan, one Bhatta Vijayan, one Bhatta Mayuran, one Bhatta Gopalan, one Bhatta Narayanan. When these 6 Sastris came, they said to the Brahmans of the place, “We will remove the troubles that the Bauddhas have brought upon you; you need not grieve at all.” At this the Brahmans were glad. They went together with the Sastris and saw the Perumal who had entered the new way, and the Sastris said, “O Perumal, what is this folly that you have committed?” They pressed the Perumal in many ways, and when he still answered, “This indeed is the truth,” the Sastris grew angry and said: “Then let the Bauddhas and us dispute upon this scripture. If it proves that we are defeated, cut out our tongues and cast us out of the land. But if it proves that the Bauddhas are defeated, cut out their tongues and drive them out of the land.” Hearing this, the Perumal agreed: “So be it.”

The Sastris and the Bauddhas disputed; the argument of the Bauddhas fell, and they were defeated. The Perumal cut out their tongues and [29] cast the rest of them out of the land. “Hereafter, when Bauddhas come and dispute, the king shall say no more than ‘Dispute, then’; and afterwards, at the word of the Vedantin,[44] he shall punish them and cast them out”: to this they made the Perumal who reigned next bind himself by covenant. For the Perumal who had entered the new way they set apart property and gave it to him, and so put him aside. Deciding, “Since I follow the Bauddha scripture, there is no resource for me in anything else,” that Perumal installed another upon the throne; and having thus governed the land for four years, he went away to Mecca.

The Bauddhas say that Cheraman Perumal went to Mecca and not to heaven. That was not Cheraman Perumal; it was Palli Bana Perumal.[45] Cheraman Perumal, the king of Kerala, went to heaven.[46] After this the reigns of four Perumals passed, and the Perumal who reigned fifth was Cheraman Perumal.

3. The Perumals who reigned down to Kulasekhara

The Brahmans went abroad and brought Tulubhan Perumal from the northern land. When that Perumal saw the country from Gokarnam as far as Perumpuzha,[47] he thought, “This kingdom is indeed a good one,” proceeded to the place called Kotisvaram,[48] and lived with the Brahmans of that gramam (Brahman settlement). Because he reigned there, [30] the country came to be called Tulunadu.[49] After he had governed for 6 years, that Perumal ascended to heaven.

Then they brought Indra Perumal and installed him. They appointed Allur to be the Great Palace (Perinkovilakam), and near it built four talis (temple-council seats) for the four kazhakams (assemblies); the particulars of that were written above, in section 1. The Taliyatiris (the Brahmans who sat in the talis) sat in council with the Perumal in the several talis. After he had governed the land for twelve years, Indra gave orders that another should be installed on the throne, and went away abroad.

Then they brought Arya Perumal from Aryapuram and installed him. Arya Perumal travelled through the kingdom of Kerala, 160 katam (leagues, of about 5 miles) of country, and surveyed it. He ordained that from Gokarnam as far as Perumpuzha in Tulunadu should be the Tulu kingdom; that from Perumpuzha as far as the bar of Putupattanam[50] should be the Kerala kingdom; that from Putupattanam as far as Kannetti[51] should be the Mushika kingdom; and that from Kannetti as far as Kanyakumari should be the Kuvala kingdom.[52] Thus he made of that country 4 divisions, of these 17 nadus (districts), and of the 17 nadus 18 tracts (kandam); he gave each desam (locality) a name, and in each desam he ordained gifts and charities. He gladdened the Brahmans, built 4 talis in the four kazhakams, and sat in council with the 4 Taliyatiris. When he had governed the land for 5 years, the gods lowered [31] a celestial car from heaven, and the Perumal went up to heaven.

After the Brahmans had suffered great distress of mind, they went abroad, brought Kundan Perumal, and installed him. That Perumal built the Vandivaka kovilakam (palace) near Kannetti, and after reigning 4 years went back abroad. Then they brought Kotti Perumal and installed him; that place came to be called Kottikkollam. He governed the land for one year and then ascended to heaven.

After that they brought Mada Perumal. When he had reigned 11 years he resolved that a fort should be built there, sent for his younger brother Ezhi Perumal, and went away abroad. Ezhi Perumal then built a fort there and named it Madayezhikotta.[53] After he had reigned 12 years came that Perumal's ascent to heaven; he went away abroad.[54]

They brought Komban Perumal and installed him. That Perumal reigned for 3 years and 6 months, living in a tent pitched on the bank of the river called Neyyura. Then Vijayan Perumal built a fort at Vijayankollam. Because Arjuna, one of the Pandavas, had stayed a long time in that place, he declared it to be Satyabhumi (land of truth).[55] After reigning 12 years he gave orders that another should be installed, [32] and Vijayan went away abroad.

The Brahmans went abroad, made Valabhan Perumal lord of Kerala, and installed him. On the bank of the river called Neyyura that Perumal saw the image of Siva set up by the sage named Sivasringan, and saw many other signs of the divine presence. He had a temple built, established certain other tutelary deities as well, built a fort there and named it Simhamukham (Lion's Face), and named the temple Sivesvaram. The fort built by order of Valabhan Perumal came to be called Valabhattattu Kotta (the fort of Valabhattam).[56] He decreed that from then on this should be the ancestral capital of those who reign in Kerala, and resolved that many ranks and usages should be ordained there. After he had reigned 11 years, that Perumal ascended to heaven.

Harischandran Perumal, who was brought after that, built Harischandra Kotta (fort) on the top of Puralimala (the Purali hill).[57] Because forest deities were often seen moving about inside that fort, other men were not able to go to the fort, see the Perumal, discuss the rights and wrongs of affairs, and come away. After this, seeing that no human being came and went there, he concluded in his royal mind that everything was filled with the divine; and after he had reigned a short time, no one saw the Perumal any more. When he was no longer to be seen, [33] the Brahmans brought Mallan Perumal. That Perumal built a fort called Mallurumallan Kotta in the Mushika kingdom, reigned 12 years, and went away abroad.

The Perumal who reigned next was Kulasekhara Perumal, from the Pandya kingdom.[58] As they were bringing him, Mahabharata Bhattatiri and Vasudeva Bhattatiri[59] met the Perumal, did him honour, and gave him their blessing. That Perumal built Chitrakutam in the Mushika kingdom and took up his residence there.

What that Perumal did was this. Holding that there ought to be good Kshatriyas, he sent for Kshatriyas and Samantas from many quarters, and cut up the country and gave it to them in portions of five katam each. These are the 5 lines of Kshatriyas and the 8 lines of Samantas.[60] The reason for it was this: if ever again a party of Bauddhas (Buddhists) should come, bewilder the king, and make their creed prevail, the Brahmans would have to go abroad.[61] Resolving that this must not happen, he set apart five katam for each of them, so that if one of them proved false, the Brahmans could withdraw and settle in another place close by. The reason was that otherwise this karmabhumi (land of sacred rites) would go to ruin; they must not have to leave it.

What Kulasekhara Perumal did further was this. Of the Sastris (men learned in the sastras, the sacred sciences) who had come, he settled Bhattacharyar and [34] Bhattabanan here, providing for their maintenance, so that the Brahmans of the Malayalam country might study the sastras. As there had been no study of the sastras before, a teacher then came from abroad along with Bhattacharyar and expounded them. That was Prabhakara Gurukkal, who composed the Prabhakara sastra.[62] After the other teachers had studied and gone, Kulasekhara Perumal, wishing that the company who studied this sastra should have some provision, built a place and gave it to these Sastris who had come. He established them there, and the Brahmans of the Malayalam country studied the sastra. Because it is the place of the Sastris it is called Bhattam. There is also a rule that this place belongs to the foremost among the Brahmans of the 64 gramams. It is commonly said that the wooden sandals of Prabhakara Gurukkal, the disciple of Bhattacharyar, are kept there.

Property of 7,000 kalams[63] was received from Kulasekhara Perumal, and property of 5,000 kalams from a Chetti (merchant) named Udayatungan, with flower and water.[64] It was not Bhattacharyar who received these twelve thousand; and because it was Prabhakara Gurukkal who received them, there is a purse (kizhi) only for Bhatta, Prabhakara and Vyakarana (grammar).[65] When the Sastris divided the brahmaswam (Brahman property), it was ruled that there was no portion for the Vedanta sastra;[66] so the Vedantis have no share in the 12,000 kalams. Because Prabhakara Gurukkal did not give to the Vedantis, at Trikkannapuram there is a purse only for Bhatta, Prabhakara and Vyakarana. Even if the Vedantis study the Vedanta, [35] they must be versed in one of the three, Bhatta, Prabhakara or Vyakarana. Among the purses at Trikkannapuram there are also some that many persons have founded since, for the sastras, and in those the Vedanti too has a share; but in the brahmaswam that Prabhakara Gurukkal received he has none.[67]

After Kulasekhara Perumal had reigned 18 years, he entered heaven in his body. The Kali chronogram of that day is “Purudhisamasrayam”.[68] The founding of the Tiruvanchakkulam shrine (mukkalvattam) also falls in the Kali date written above.[69] That Kali date is A.D. 333.

4. How the rakshapurushans and the Brahmans ruled

Afterwards, when the Perumal had gone to heaven, they resolved, “To protect the land is a hard matter; for the Brahmans nothing will do unless the Brahmans have strength in their own hands.” For the sake of protection the Brahmans of the 64 gramams, being of one mind, appointed 10½ gramams. The 10½ gramams that were to take up the sword are: Perumanam, Iringadikkoda, Chovara, Alattur, Karikkadu, Payyanur, Tiruvilvayi, Trissivaperur, Airanikkulam, Mushikakkulam, and half of Kazhutanadu.[70] Thus at the sacred hall of Trikkariyur[71] all 64 gramams came together as under one shade, and when the assembly was complete and the appointment had been made, those men became rakshapurushans (protectors), with the name of Sastris.

For the touching of the sword they fixed upon a place within the four mandalams in all, and coming to Angikkal in one mandalam [36] they took up arms.[72] Only 8½ gramams took them up together. Avattiputtur and Ettumanur, though they had joined in the appointment, hung back.[73][74] A second time, when all had received them and taken them up, they too took them up. In particular, when all the 64 gramams made the covenant, the covenant was this: “Those who have touched these arms shall cause the rites to be performed and shall protect dharma. So that the taint of having taken up arms may be set aside, they shall be joined with us in meals, in the punyaham (rite of purification), and in the affairs of the caste.” All those of these 10½ gramams were made bearers of arms, and so they stand appointed. These ten gramams are called Chattirar. Because they made a gift of the rites prescribed in the sastra, the name is Sastran.[75] They hold the sword in hand in order to protect.

Apart from those gramams, in 3 gramams all who go by the name of arms-bearers are reckoned with the unarmed. In the sastra it stood of old that, among those of a gramam, the arms-bearers and the unarmed should meet together as one company. At the time of this appointment the one who was Kshatriya was at the Airul kovilakam as witness. Those who became Chattirar were eight gramams; and of those appointed to take up these arms, those who did take them up also stand appointed.[76] The body of those who, without taking up arms, do not go out even for deeds of valour[77] stand in the place of the unarmed. A certain few remain without going out, in order that the sacrifices and other rites may not be given up; [37] those who took up arms are to cause them to be performed, and they too have the fruit of the performance. Those who remain must together hold the vessel of the punyaham. The other gramams and those of the 10 gramams stand joined together, hand in hand.

Those who have taken up arms will break off their rites and break off their purifications; in every case that arises it is this body of men that is the protector, as the overlord. When it comes about that they cannot protect, they give up their lives: they die of set purpose, and so they protect; and then the funeral rite with mantras must not be left undone for them; it must be done.[78] The unarmed must not act in this way. At the Trikkanna kazhakam 2 Adhyans died.[79] At the Iringanikkuda kazhakam, Pushkarappadu and Vellikuda of Mattedattu Vanam are counted among those who died. They died on the day of Punartam in the month of Chingam;[80] on that day there is a sraddha by the whole gramam. On that day the funeral rite with mantras was done for them. Because some of the Ten-and-a-half died, it is said to this day that at Trikkannapuram 2 are wanting.

As for the body of those in the arms-bearing gramams who stay in their houses without going out even to have their share made good: if it should happen that they resolve, “From now on we too will fall in battle,” then when those who did not go out before do go out, the rule is that the assembly must meet in this manner and that they must set out from such-and-such a place. When they set out for the very first time they may not do it as they please. That is what it is to go about bearing arms; otherwise they are merely as one of the unarmed. As everything else goes on alike, they are but one. The house-names of the managers of the unarmed [38] became names for the Sastrar also.[81]

The endowment of the Sastris is what Prabhakara Gurukkal received. For the Chattirar the endowment is that very sixth share which all the 64 gramams together gave of old, to be enjoyed by the rakshapurushans for the protection of Kerala.[82] Foremost among them, the Prabhakaras of the Mangattu kuru (faction): Panichikkadu, Karamangalam, Putuva and Mana. The Bhattas in the Mangattu kuru: Auvanikkada, Venmaniyachi, Yamanam; Vyakarana: Putuva, Neduntirutti, Palekkettu. The Prabhakaras in the Vellangallur kuru: Venmani, Vediyur; its Bhattam: Putuva, Palekkadu, Karamangalam; of the same, Velullur, in the Kara division of Karamangalam; Bhatta and Vyakarana: Adiya, Manachokkadu, Tazhappalli. Of these, the Prabhakara of the northern Mangattu kuru: Varavakkattu; Bhattam: Nenmani, Nitamaram, Chovvaram; Pullu Kanda Puli, Vyakarana.[83] There is much more that could be told.

Four things are of chief importance to the rakshapurushans: the kanam (the sitting in session), the kalikkuttam (the company for the games), and the sanghalakshanam (the marks of the company); these 3 existed from before. The planting of the flag at Tirunavayi came in as the fourth.[84] For the kalikkuttam all four varnas are needed together.[85] When the lads of the kalikkuttam make their circuit, a Brahman must go round holding the chermangalam (gong). When the Taliyatiris made covenant with the 3 varnas, the covenant was that these should join in doing the rites which they themselves do. Later, when the rakshapurushans made covenant, it was that in the rites done by Brahmans the other [39] 3 varnas also might take part. So it is said that there were 2 rounds, and that covenant was made with the 3 varnas; only with the 2 together is it complete. At Paravur, the Vaisya kazhakam, covenant is made with the Vaisya; at Mushikakkulam, which is the Kshatriya kazhakam, with the Kshatriya; at Iringanikkuda, which is for sacrifice, with the Brahman; and at Airanikkulam, which is the Sudra kazhakam, with the Sudra.[86] The Sudra, who is the support of all this, must offer his bali (ritual offering of food) together with the Brahman, in the Brahman's portion of the bali.

Then the rakshapurushans proceed to Tirunavayi and, as has been proclaimed, mount the platform, plant the flag, and sing the flag-song; and standing on the platform they proclaim how they have been made pure by the heat of the sun. The flag-song is this: “Sabhyah sravata panditah kavikale, manyah mahalokare, viprah sajjanasanghare, sapataya praudhascha bhupalare, chollunnengale turupuradetenna enningine ellavarum chevi tannu kelkkanitaram, ellarkkum esho'njalih” (Hear, you of the assembly: learned men and poets, honoured great folk, Brahmans and companies of the good, and you mighty kings! We declare …; let all lend their ears and listen. To all, this our salutation with joined hands).[87][88] This flag-song must be sung in the raga (melodic mode) called Bahuladhuli. When the rakshapurushans set out, they must take off the sacred thread, at the taking up of arms.[89]

Next is told how the kanam sits. For the sitting of a kanam, a temple in which some other estate also has a right of ownership will not do. When it is heard that one of the 6 sanghams (companies) has sat in kanam, no other sangham thinks of issuing an invitation. When a kanam is about to sit, those whose standing in hall and kitchen is in doubt do not sit along with the rakshapurushans.[90] [40] When setting out to sit in kanam, each must bring together in one place those of his own kanam circle (kanappuram) who have authority in the kanam, and must inform his masters, the men of his faction, and the lords; with their consent, all those of the kanam circle must be there. When the men, leaving out those whose standing in hall and kitchen is in doubt, have settled that on such a day they sit in kanam in such a temple, they come to another place on the night of the day before. The master of the sangham sets a lamp, seats each man apart, chooses him formally and takes him by the hand. All together they bathe and eat, smear themselves with sandal paste, tie on the waist-cloth and the head-cloth, and, with instruments sounding and lamps carried before them, they must go to the temple where the kanam is to sit. On the way there must be mock wrestling, war games and the like.

They go three times round the temple, then go inside, lay down their arms, and bow to the god. On the day itself they rise early at the temple, bathe and perform their ablutions, go inside, and have the pujas (acts of worship) begun. If there has been no siveli there before, there must be one on that day. It is laid down that there must be a sribhutabali as well;[91] only when the pujas have been done in this manner may they sit. The Chattirar must also remain in purity of body from the day before. They may chew betel and may smear sandal paste; but until the kanam that is sitting is over there must be no shaving and no intercourse with women. The cloth must be worn tucked between the legs. The nirmalyam (flowers and offerings taken off the image) and what belongs to the daytime must be avoided.[92]

After the puja they set in the temple a standing lamp [41] and a Ganapati,[93] set down a measure of paddy and a measure of rice,[94] and sit in a circle round the lamp. Then, meditating on “protection and punishment”, they are to sit in one place and inquire into and learn the concord and the particulars needed for protecting; and further how matters have been disposed of, the means by which the rites of the Brahmans may go on without hindrance, and, if there is said to be a fault in some place, the clearing away of the suspicion that has arisen. All this they are to consider and to speak of, sitting in one place in concord; it is for this that they sit. The lamp that has been set must not go out until the kanam is over. The people connected with it should keep the tappu (drum) and the chermangalam set beside the lamp. Then all together, murmuring their assent, are to rise from there and stand; with waist-cloth and head-cloth tied on, smeared with sandal, and holding the pipe in the hand, they must stand at the doorway, with the words “May it be well”, for as long as the standing lasts.[95]

They must not be under diksha (the vow of going unshaven, as in mourning). There must be no touching of Ambalavasis (temple servants). When they sit to eat, for the Kshatriya a separate lamp is set and a leaf softened over the fire is laid, and he is served with honour, but only so; his food must also be cooked apart. So as to avoid laying a leaf for him in the row at the serving, a shed is to be put up outside the temple.[96] If that place is not large enough for the feast, a house is to be built outside. They are to keep up their daily exercises. They may take up and wield arms, and may practise fitting music and the drumming for the games; they may study literary compositions. Drumming for the goddess and the dancing of courtesans are not allowed. The Maharayar [42] may do these things without taking off the sacred thread. With the sacred thread taken off, nothing may be undertaken. The circuit round the lamp is the expiation for everything. The number of days is the same as that of the sannyasi's chaturmasyam; it must begin on the day the chaturmasyam begins.[97] A Sudra must not be touched knowingly. No Sudras other than the sweepers and sprinklers and the Marayars (temple drummers) may enter the temple. A kanam is held only in a Brahman temple; there must be no kanam during pula (the pollution that follows a death in the family). For the kanam there is no difference between south and north. The arms-bearers rank below the unarmed who made the covenant.[98]

After that the several classes of the gramams were distinguished and ordained. Among the temples of the Malayalam country: Gokarnam, Trissivaperur, Tirunavayi, Trikkariyur, Trikkannapuram, Tiruvanchikkulam, Iringanikkuda, Airanikkulam, Vellappanadu, and Angikkal in Mandalam; within these 10 places the covenant is made.[99] Eleven gramams have the soma offering:[100] Chovaram, Perumanam, Iringanikkuda, Alattur, Mushikakkulam, Uliyannur, Chenganodu, Perinchellur, Karikkadu, Paiyanur; only these have the soma offering.[101] Among them, foremost for the soma offering are Perinchellur, Karikkadu, Alattur, Perumanam, Chovaram and Iringanikkuda; these six are acknowledged alike. In the other classes precedence belongs to the body of the ur (village). For those of the desam he ordained the six works: sacrificing, teaching, recitation of the Veda, alms, giving, and the receiving of gifts.[102] The persons who have these hold the 6 offices of teacher. To them were also granted connection with the temples, [43] precedence at the rites for the ancestors in Kerala, and the name of Desikal.

Then to those of the sabha (assembly) were granted, in the chief temples between Kanyakumari and Gokarnam, the pattam (rent), the samudayam (management), the santi (priestly service)[103], the hall, the kitchen, the ambalappadi (temple steps), and the urayma (trusteeship): these six privileges (the office of Periya Nambi was also granted).[104] For the office of lawgiver of the Sixty-four, 2 persons were appointed. One person, of the Puliyampadappu house in Perinchellur gramam, was given the names of Prabhu (lord) and Nayaka (leader) of the 64, and was granted full control in all the 64. Then, so that if a dispute should arise among them concerning the standing and the observances ordained for the 64 it might be settled and things carried on, one person in Alattur gramam was appointed; the Azhavancheri sovereignty (samrajyam) was ordained, he was given the name of Samprakkal, and he was declared the lawgiver for the Brahmans.[105] These two are the chief of the Brahmans in Kerala.

As for the rest, each tells it in his own way in his own place; the practice is not uniform. In the great temples more is seen in the 6 gramams of Kurumbanadu; it is those of the 6 desams who have the more.[106] In Kurumbanadu[107] the 6 gramams and 4 desams are joined together as one: Kulambadi, Ramanallur, Karusseri, Chattamangalam, these make one; Ozhiyadi, Uzhutamannur, Talapperuman, these make one; Kuzhakkodu, Nellikkadu, Chalappuram, Chattanellur, Cherumannur, Parappur, Cherumam, Manappuram, these make one.

5. The story of Krishna Rayar and Cheraman Perumal

[44] After the Brahmans of the 64 gramams and the Perumals had thus together protected the land for a short time, all the 64 gramams met in full assembly. At the temple of Trikkariyur and on the sandbank of Tirunavayi, with every head present, the solemn council deliberated: “It is not enough to have ordained things in this way; the country will be left without punishment and protection. The Brahmans will have to quit the country. There must be a king.” So resolving with one mind, they went abroad (they met Krishna Rayar of Anakundi, stated the term, namely that he should send someone to govern Kerala for twelve years at a time, and made many covenants and oaths).[108] He appointed and sent Adi Raja Perumal to reign for one term of twelve years, and after him Pandi Perumal. When their reigns were over, he chose and fixed upon Cheraman Perumal, a Kshatriya.[109]

As they were bringing Cheraman Perumal, they met Vasudeva Mahabhattatiri as an omen, went on with him in company, and sat in council beneath the Ponmadam (golden upper hall) of Trikkariyur. The Brahmans of the 64 gramams gave him the writ of command, so that he might hold and rule the 160 katam of the kingdom of Kerala, and gave him flower and water, so that he might be installed as sole lord under one umbrella;[110] and Cheraman Perumal received with water the 160 katam of the kingdom of Kerala. [45] The Kali chronogram of that day is “Svargasandehaprapyam”.[111] A.D. 428.

After that Cheraman Perumal travelled through the 160 katam of country and surveyed it, and declared Trikkariyur, the sandbank of Tirunavayi, and Valabhattattu Kotta, these three desams, to be Satyabhumi. On the right-hand side of Valabhattattu Kotta he built Cheraman Kotta. Then he viewed all the 18 river-mouths, found the Tiruvanchazhi mouth to be the chief of them, and built the temple called Tiruvanchakkulam. In the great temples where many Perumals had sat in council, Cheraman Perumal too sat in council with the Brahmans. After he had governed well in this way for 12 years, the Brahmans, seeing how greatly the Perumal excelled in virtues, resolved, “Krishna Rayar appointed Cheraman Perumal with a stated term, to reign for 12 years; but that is not binding,” and appointed Cheraman Perumal himself to govern the land for another 12 years.

Seeing that Rajalakshmi (the fortune of kingship) and Viryalakshmi (the fortune of valour) shone very brightly in Cheraman Kotta, he took up his residence there, set up a door-frame, and within the Cheraman door-frame ordained many ranks and usages. He had the royal lady who had been brought from abroad married to a Brahman, and decreed that the offspring born of her should be Kshatriya.[112] The Brahmans too held that there ought to be good Solar Kshatriyas[113] in this Kerala, [46] and property was set apart for them. So for that Kshatriya lady he built a kovilakam near Chitrakutam in the Mushika kingdom, where Kulasekhara Perumal had reigned, and settled her there. Two sons were born of her; the elder he appointed to Chitrakutam and the younger to Tulunadu.

While Cheraman Perumal was reigning in Cheraman Kotta, in the northern land two Vellala women[114] and a royal lady came together to bathe on the bank of a river called Malini. Catching the fragrance of flowers, all three got into a boat to pick the flowers; the head of the boat went astray, they were carried out to sea, and they came to land at the foot of Ezhimala.[115] The three got out of the boat, climbed up on the hill, and stood there. When Cheraman Perumal learnt the news, he commanded that they should be brought. He had a lamp and a plank seat set in the carpeted hall, put rice in a golden dish, and stood waiting. The three came inside Cheraman Kotta. One of the women went straight towards the hall of audience and made as if to set one foot upon it, but did not step up into the carpeted hall, and stood on the right-hand side of where the Tampuran (lord) was. The other woman did not come straight to where the Tampuran stood, but went round and stood on the Tampuran's left. The third, [47] the royal lady, came straight up to the Tampuran, stepped up into the hall of audience, made her obeisance in due form, and sat down. Seeing that, the Perumal took up rice from the golden dish and three times with his royal hand sprinkled it on her,[116] and pronounced that the offspring born of her should be Ezhibhupan (king of Ezhi); he also decreed that they should have the right to this kingdom, and had the Ezhottu kovilakam built at the foot of Ezhimala for the lady to reside in. She who came straight (nerittu) is the Nerpatta swarupam (ruling house); she who went round (chuzhannu) is the Chuzhali swarupam.[117]

Then he gave orders that trade should be carried on from the 18 river-mouths of the Malayalam country, and sent for many merchants and Jonakar (Muslims) and settled them.[118] From the city called Velapuram in Aryapuram, the land where the Perumal was born, he sent for a Jonakan and a Jonaka woman and settled them at Aryappadi. Because the good place where they were to be settled was as an eye (kannu) to the country, he gave it the name Kannanur, and also Velapuram. He styled the Jonakan Azhi Raja and the woman Umma, and installed them with the sprinkling of rice.[119] Afterwards, seeing ever more of the Perumal's surpassing virtues, the Brahmans resolved that they could not part with Cheraman Perumal.

So when the king named Cheraman had reigned 36 years,[120] Krishna Rayar, declaring, “The Brahmans have not come abroad, nor have I seen Cheraman Perumal,” gathered an army in order to subdue the Malayalam country.

[48] Afterwards the Brahmans went to the Chola country, brought the king named Cheraman, and anointed and crowned him.[121] After he had protected the land for 12 years, because the Kali age, now begun, was gaining strength, the Brahmans and the subjects living there divided into two parties and disputed; and on account of the virtues of Cheraman Perumal they gave up the rule laid down by Sri Parasurama and consented that Cheraman Perumal himself should go on protecting Kerala. Because the rule of Parasurama was given up, it came about that the 64 gramams no longer met as one. While Cheraman Perumal was thus ruling, the Rayar, who was king of Pandi, resolved: “If the king of the Chola country is to hold the Malayalam country, which ought to be protected jointly, then half of Kerala must be brought under us.” He climbed the Anamala (Elephant Hills),[122] came down into the forest, and built a fort and made it fast. When Cheraman Perumal heard of this, he summoned to Trikkadamatilakam all his chekavans (fighting men) in Kerala and the chief captains among them, completed the muster, proceeded to Taravur nadu,[123] and ordered that the Rayar's fort should be destroyed. But though they strove in many ways, they did not succeed in destroying the Rayar's fort, and Cheraman Perumal was distressed.

Then the Brahmans and the Perumal deliberated in the solemn council beneath the Ponmadam of Trikkariyur, in the Sri Nava temple (the temple of Tirunavayi); [49] they brought together the fighting men of the 17 nadus and had those services done to the gods that would give victory in battle. When they then inquired who there was that had the fortune of conquest in every quarter, they found that the Tampuran named Udayavarman of the Karippattu kovilakam, the son of the Kshatriya lady, had that fortune; and they found that if Manichan and Vikkiran[124] of Puntura, two Eradis[125], were brought, the battle would be won.[126] To fetch them they wrote a token and sent it by the hand of Arya Brahmans.

These went to Puntura and inquired, and were told that they were at the writing-school. Going there they found the two Eradis and the master who was teaching them to write, Todavakkalattu Unnikkumara Nambiyar; they delivered the written token and explained the matter. As they were all coming along together, at Venchalapparambu, in the avenue of banyans, at the foot of a kanjiram tree,[127] they saw the Azhavancheri Tamprakkal sitting facing east with his umbrella laid upside down, and the Digvara Nambutiri of that house, and they bowed down before them. The Tamprakkal asked them, “Where are you going?” The master answered, “Your servants are on their way there, in company with the Arya Brahmans who were sent from the solemn council of Trikkariyur.” [50] Hearing that, the Tamprakkal said, “We too have set out for that very place.” When they prostrated themselves at full length, he was pleased and gave his pledge: “May good come to you hereafter! I shall see that the business you are going upon succeeds.”

On the road, as they went on from there, 7 cows were lying dead. Beside one of the cows 14 vultures were sitting; they had not touched any of the others. When the Tamprakkal saw this he asked, “O vultures! Of the 7 cows lying dead, what is the reason that you leave six of them untouched?” Then a lame cock vulture shook out its wings, plucked out a feather with its beak, and gave it to him. When he took it in his hand and looked at the cows, only one of them had been born with the birth of a cow; all the others he saw to be births of various other beasts.[128] The two Eradis and the Nambiyar he saw to be of human birth. The Tamprakkal put that feather into the Nambiyar's hand and explained to him how it was to be used. The Eradis and the Nambiyar bowed down at the Tamprakkal's feet and received his blessing. It is for that reason that to this day, when the Kunnalakonatiri Raja sees the Azhavancheri Tamprakkal, he must join his royal hands in salutation.[129]

Setting out from there they came to the solemn council at Trikkariyur and made their obeisance. When they asked the Brahmans and Cheraman Perumal, “What is the business for which you sent for us?” the answer was: [51] “Krishna Rayar of Anakundi has come to war, with all his preparations made, to subdue the Malayalam country. Against this the fighting men of the 17 nadus have been brought together and are kept in readiness. You must go along with them, win the battle, and come back.” When this command was given they agreed, “So be it,” saluted the council, and came away.

While Cheraman Perumal was engaged in worship of the Lord, two Samantas, of the Samantas born in the Solar race, proud heroes known as Puntura, went together to Rameswaram, bathed at the Setu, and on their way to Kasi came to the Sri Nava temple.[130] While they were staying there, the Brahman renowned by the name of Tolan, who was the Perumal's favourite minister,[131] was telling various news to the Samantas, who had come as wayfarers; and in the course of it he told them how the Rayar had built a fort in order to subdue the Malayalam country, and how Cheraman Perumal had been turned back in battle. He was greatly pleased with the truthfulness and the good character of Manavikraman. Then, after the Brahmans and the Perumal had gone into the water,[132] they were giving orders how to go and conquer the Rayar, and so he told the Samantas of it. The two Samantas considered together and said to the Brahmans, “If you send us with them, we will fight and shake the Rayar's fort.”

After this had been reported to the Perumal also, the two were brought [52] and seated with honour; and for many days, for the sake of mutual trust, their understanding, their strength and their prowess were put to the proof. When it was known that the Samantas were mighty in the art of war, the fighting men were summoned without delay and the muster was completed. The Perumal's 122 captains, and with them nine hundred thousand chekavar, held it for certain that this body, which is ours only on tenure, does not last; and the Perumal sent them with the Samantas, and appointed and sent Kizhur Unnikkumara Menon and Parakkal Uravinkal Para Chankara Nambi to write the accounts.

On the way to the war, at night, the Samantas went to where the warriors were sleeping and walked three times round the host. Holding the vulture's feather in the hand, they looked, and put a mark of silver on each of those who had been born with human birth: there were 10,000 Nayars born with human birth, 30,000 born with the birth of gods, and the rest of the host were seen to be of the birth of asuras (demons).[133] They had rings put on the 10,000 Nayars and came away. Knowing by their marks those who were heroes even in sleep, they put marks on their weapons with gopi (yellow earth) and with sandal paste, and then, without anyone having perceived it, stayed in their own places. These 10,000 Nayars and the Nambiyar together took the battle on the right wing. The Perumal's ministers, the 11 other than Padamala Nayar, together took the battle on the left wing. [53] The force on the left wing gave way and came back; the force on the right wing marched on, left the Malayalam country, went into the foreign land and made its battlefield fast, and it turned back the enemy, fought, conquered, and came home.

The Samantas made 30,000 Nayars who carried bows and arrows march in front, and behind them made the 10,000 Nayars march, men trained in all the 18 weapons, of the upper kiriyam,[134] excelling in unity, in valour, and in the standing of Nayar leadership. Going with the 32 captains, they went up outside the Rayar's fort, broke into the camp that was pitched there, fought all that day, did great damage to many elephants, horses and foot soldiers, cleared the camp, and drove them inside the fort. In the night Manachan and Vikraman took counsel together. They posted half of the 10,000 Nayars at the north gate of the fort, posted the rest firmly on the 4 sides, fought day and night for 3 days, drove out the Rayar, and took the fort.

That day the Perumal gave to all the rewards that were due, dismissed the host, seated the elder of the Samantas on his royal lap, and had the hero's chain put on his right arm and his right leg. He gave orders that the 10,000 Nayars should be settled in the most pleasing district in Kerala. The ministers said that they should be settled in Polanadu;[135] so the subjects who were there were removed from it, the towns of the district were cleared, [54] and what they had built was shifted as well. One company was settled in Edakkazhi nattutara, the Nayars of the attam at Iringadikkodu,[136] and the chief men among the picked Nayars in the desam of Kozhikode; and the ministers reported to the Perumal how they had been settled. He summoned the Manavikramans to the royal presence and declared, “I have resolved to make you two my heirs and to establish your rule here.” They answered, “We are going to Kasi; when we have bathed in the Ganga, carried the kavadi (pole with pots of Ganga water) to Rameswaram, and both come back here, we shall do as is to be done.” Having submitted this, they went off to Kasi.

6. Particulars of the order of castes ordained by Sankaracharyar

At the time when the war had been won, there appeared a divine being, of very great renown, as the son[137] of Sri Mahadeva; it was he who afterwards became Sankaracharyar.[138] Hear how that came about. After a Brahman woman had been widowed, at a time when she lay under suspicion of a “kitchen fault” (a breach of chastity) and had been put out of caste, Sri Mahadeva came and begot a child on her. By the Lord's compassion he came down and was born as her son: Sankaracharyar of Sringeri.[139] When he had studied a little learning, his mother died; [55] and because at that juncture the Brahmans did not come for the rites, he made a pit for the sacrificial fire in his own house, heaped up the pyre, kindled the fire, cut up the corpse, offered it in the fire, and burnt it.[140] The rites that the next of kin should do he had done by a Sudra, by one who stands next to the Brahmans. Because he had thus burnt her himself, he ruled that without a Brahman there is no rite for a Sudra, and without a Sudra there is no rite for a Brahman.

When Sankaracharyar had come to have learning in abundance, there was no one equal to him, and the Brahmans could no longer stand before him. Having mastered all the sciences and become renowned, at the time when he had ascended the Sarvajna Pitham (seat of omniscience) and was seated there, he, at the bidding of Govinda Sannyasi,[141] set out the state of the land of Kerala in 24,000 granthas.[142] He summoned the 64 gramams and ordained ranks and usages, law and standing, the distinctions of the castes, propriety and due order, the rules of leavings and of what has fallen, of pollution by approach and of bathing,[143] of marking off the well to draw water and marking off the pot to cook and eat, and for each their several occupations, usages and forms of speech, as befitted each caste. Of the four varnas he made 18 castes, and from these he ordained what are called the 68 caste-varnas and the 72 castes.

Hear the names of those castes one by one. Even within the four varnas that begin with the Brahman there are many names, and among the Brahmans themselves there are many: Ottanmar (reciters of the Veda), Mantravadikal (masters of spells), Smartanmar (authorities on the law-books), [56] Sastrangakkar (men of the branches of the sastras), Pitrukarmakkar (performers of ancestral rites), Granthikal (men of books), Jyotishakkar (astrologers), Sharikal, Vyakaranakkar (grammarians), Santikkar (officiating priests), Sastrikal, Vedantikal, Vaidikanmar, Grihasthanmar (householders) and Sannyasikal (ascetics). Because Brahman women stay within the house and do not go out, they came to be called Antarjanam (“inside-folk”) and Akattammamar (“ladies of the interior”). Brahman boys are called Unni and the girls Tangalpillamar.

The Brahmans who came from Aryavartta[144] are called Nambutirimar, Namburippadu, Embranmar and Embrantiri, and the leading men among them are called, by way of respect, Tirumulpadanmar[145] and Bhattatirippadu. From their performing the various sacrifices and other such rites they are spoken of as Somatirimar and Agnihotrikal.[146] The foreign Brahmans are the Bhattanmar, that is, the Pattar.[147] These are Vaidikas.

Since the Nambidi has no recitation of the Veda he is three-quarters of a Brahman. The chief among them, the Nambidi called Kakkattu Karanappadu, takes up arms and serves as escort; for the worship of his ancestors he has the darbha grass, the sruva ladle and the chamata sticks.[148] The Nambidi of Venganadu, who was brought in from outside, is a lord who sits with the assembly of Brahmans on the avanappalaka (the plank seat used by Brahmans). Lower than he is the Karuka Nambidi. The Nambidi follows marumakkattayam (inheritance through the sister's children).

Next come those of the intermediate class, the Ambalavasikal (temple-dwellers): those who have risen from the Sudra and those who have come down from the Brahman.[149] Among them the Pothuvals are of two kinds. The Akappothuval (inner Pothuval) is to carry the tidambu (processional image) at the bali offering to Siva, [57] to guard and take care of the temple property, the temple and the god, and to wash the temple steps. The Purappothuval (outer Pothuval) is to receive the offerings and hand them on, and to make ready leaves, firewood, milk, honey, ghee and the like. Those who perform the Sakti-puja in the service of Bhagavati bear various names: Pidaranmar, Pisharakanmar, and Adiyanmar or Adikal. The Pushpakan, and the Nambiyachan too, is to give flowers to the god, string garlands and do the service of the temple; his wife is called Brahmini. His house is called Pumatham and Padodakam, and he himself is called Punambi. The Brahmini's livelihood is simply to sing at the marriage rites.

To the Pisharodi he assigned the usage of a sannyasi, together with sweeping and sprinkling in the temple and the stringing of garlands.[150] He appointed the Kailasavasi (“dweller on Kailasa”) to the service of the temple; for the woman placed with him he made sweeping and sprinkling the chief duty and gave her the name Variyatti; and to the Vari caste[151] he assigned the same pollution and purification as the Kshatriyas, and the work of the Pushpakan. All who are born and bred in this class are said to belong to the Azhavancheri Tamprakkal.[152]

Among the Slaghyar (“the honoured”) the man is called Chakyar and the woman Nangyar. They are to set forth and tell the stories of the gods, to read grammar, dramas and puranas, to perform the kuttu and to have the kuttu spoken.[153] For their many rites he appointed a company called Charnnavar (dependants); these are the Nambiyar. Among them the Elayathu is to have the rice cooked at the sraddha of Sudras and to receive it.[154] The Mussad, the Urile parisha (“the party of the village”), [58] are Tangals; because they took upon themselves the guilt of Parasurama they have no Brahman rites at all.[155] The Chelampandis, who go with these, are servants of the Lord at Tiruvantapuram.[156] To dance before Sasta he appointed a class called Tiyadi Nambi, and there is also a dependent class called Taiyambadi; these are the people who draw the kalam and sing the deity.[157] Where the Adiyanmar of Bhadrakali hold their worship he appointed, for the duties of kazhakam (here, temple service), two companies of dependants, Manari and Puttillam. These and the Unittirimar are counted among the Ambalavasis.

The Marayar are not counted among the Ambalavasis; they are players of instruments. The Kottumarayar (drummer Marayar), the Asthikurachi and the Asthivari (“bone-gatherer”), because they give attendance at the burning of the dead, are of the Pariyarattavar (attendants). All these are antarajatis, intermediate castes falling between the four varnas.

Among the Kshatriyas there are the Solar line and the Lunar line; in these two kinds there are the Mushika Kshatriya, the Mudi (crowned) Kshatriya and the Samantas.[158] The Eradi, the Nedungadi and the Vallodi are said to be a lower class among them, and are called Adiyodis. Ever since Mayuravarman ruled Malayalam and the Taulava (Tulu) country, the names of all the kings who have arisen end in Varman or Sarman.[159]

Some say that there are Vaisyas in the Malayalam country and some say that there are not; there are some in Vayanadu.

To the Brahmans belong the Vedas and sastras, sacrifices and such rites, [59] and propitiatory rites such as japa and homa (muttered prayer and fire-offering). To the Kshatriyas belong kingship, protection and punishment, and the care of the subjects. To the Vaisya belong tillage, the keeping of cattle and trade. To the Sudra belong war, hunting, service for the three-nazhi ration (munnazhippadu), guard and escort.[160] To those below them belong squeezing out tali (a herbal wash for the hair) and bathing people, carrying the palanquin, tying up loads, strewing sesame, dyeing fine cloths, and other hired service.

The Sudra castes are spoken of in many ways. Among them, in the Vellala kind, the names are Tangal, Kammal, Kuruppu, Panikkar, Nayakan, Nayar, Adiyodi, Nambiyar, Chellattanmar, Talachennor, Talappennor, Menokki, Menon, Appan, Ammonmar and Ammavan; in each swarupam (ruling house) a different name is used.[161] All these taravadu (matrilineal family) houses are sprung from 11 kiriyams[162]: 1. Mutukkiriyam, 2. Ilankiriyam, 3. Adunkudi-kiriyam, 4. Amayangalattu-kiriyam, 5. Edattukudi-kiriyam, 6. Nelluli-kiriyam, 7. Nilancheri-kiriyam, 8. Idima-kiriyam, 9. Mambadu-kiriyam, 10. Tirumangalattu-kiriyam, 11. Puttur-kiriyam.[163]

The classes that have sunk below these are the dependent classes. There are dependants attached to the four varnas; and because it is said that the Samantas too have dependants, some speak of five kinds and some of four. The Akattu Charnnavar (inner dependants), Purattu Charnnavar (outer dependants), Parappuvar, Prabhusevakar (servants of lords), Pallichekavar, Pallichanmar and Madavar: such are the classes who have to stand and do service in the temple, in the edam (chief's house), in the matham (religious house), [60] in the madam (mansion) and in the kovilakam (palace). They are the Ullalar, the Ullattil Nayar and the Ullakattu Nayanmar, and in Eranadu and elsewhere the Kulichekavar (hired retainers). The Pallichanmar are those who of old bore the palanquins of the Taliyatirimar.[164]

The castes below these are as follows. The Veluttedan, Irankolli and Vannattan: to wash cloths, wring them out and deliver them, and to pleat the sacred garment. The Vilakkattaravan and Valinchiyan: shaving, and the ancestral rites. The Kusavan, Kulalan, Koyappan and Antyan: to make earthen pots. The Urali, Kalleri Nayar, Manayalikal and Eruman: to build walls, lay upper floors, cut down hillocks, fill up pits, dig tanks and wells, and dig for hire. The Vattakkattavan (the Vaniyan; the Patiyar and the Chakkala Vaniyan are to press sesame in the mill). These make 5 castes.[165]

Then the Kudumbar and Kaduppattar: to tie up loads and to sell salt and fish. The Kacheri Nayar: to set up a shop and trade; he and the Vattakkattavan are one and the same. The Nayikkanmar: to summon the assembly by beat of drum. The Kuttan Nayar and Kandattil Nayar: to pound rice in the temple, scour the vessels and guard the gate-tower. These are people who have sunk from the dependent class.

The Akattuttu parisha.[166] Besides the Kacheri Chettiyan there are 3 kinds of traders, the Ravari, Yavari and Vyapari: sailing ships, building warehouses and trading in them, and dealing by giving goods to the seafarers and taking goods from them. The Chetti: trade in gold; changing gold when coin has been struck at the mint; having the Marakkar of the port fit out ships for Mecca; voyaging, trade [61] and the writing of accounts.[167] The Jonakar, who are Bauddhas, sprung from the race of the Asuras: trade and sailing ships.[168] Then the Chinese, the Kuncharattikkar (men of Gujarat) and the Pauravar; these came by ship from one island or another and are settled in the Malayalam country. Among these there are 18 races: the Konginiyar, the Cherippukutti (shoemakers), the Nasrani, the Ottanmar, the Pauran and so on.[169] The Parangis (Portuguese), the Lanta (Dutch), the Parintiris (French) and the Inkiris (English), these four wearers of the round hat, have crossed over and settled in one island or another, built forts and made them fast, and begun to trade.[170]

The Chaliyar are people who came from foreign parts, built streets and began to weave; so too the Chettiyar and the Chedar. The Izhavar and the Tiyar[171] came from the island called Izham[172]: climbing trees, sawing and cutting, distilling, and trade; among them there is the office of Tandan (headman).[173][174] The Kavutiyan is the barber who goes with them. The Mukarvar or Mukayar: to catch fish in the rivers. The Mukkuvar also, and the Kadavar: to catch fish with nets, to ferry boats and to carry burdens; they are said to have come from Izham.

The Kammalar or Karmalar are called the Five, the Ainkudi (five households), and also the Four.[175] Among them the Brahman is the Achari or Asari: to fell timber and shape it. The Kshatriya is the Tattan or Peruntattan: to make ornaments and images. The Chozhi Tattan: to enter the mint and strike coin; the Ponvani and the Chakrakuttiyar have the work of stamping. The Vaisya is the Muchari or Musari: work in bell-metal, casting vessels of worship and other things. [62] The Sudra is the Kollan or Perunkollan: work in iron. The Chembukotti or Chembotti: work in copper.

Those who parted from the Kammalar and went their own way are four kinds of Kollar. Among them are the Tikollan or Karuvan; the Ambuketti Kollan or Padakkuruppu: to smooth bows, fit arrows and train men in arms; the Palisakkollan or Kidaran: to make and supply shields, and leather work; the Valkollan (Kadacha Kollan): to burnish weapons and hand them over. There are also four Kuruppu who do no hired soldiering: the Vadikuruppu, to make spear-shafts; the Parakuruppu, to make lime; the Paravan or Kattukkuruppu; and the Velakkuruppu. The Velan and the midwife: healing, attending at childbirth, the use of the knife and the rites of the lying-in woman. The Panar, Munnuttan, Anjuttan, Velan and Paravan: to climb trees, smear the floor for the kalam, dance in costume (kettiyattam), lay demons, undo odi sorcery, and spell-craft.[176]

Standing as bondsmen to the Kammalar there are 4 kinds: the Mankutti, Maramkayari, Kodanchi and Kottamutti; these are one and the same. The Vannan, Mannan and Peruvannan: supplying the change of clean cloth (ettu and mattu), dancing in costume, warding off the rice-bug and the worm, spell-craft and needlework.[177] Then the Kanisan or Kaniyan: astrology, spell-craft, handing over the weapons at the nalpattiradi (the fencing ground of forty-two feet), the office of master in the kalari (school of arms), and informing the assembly.[178][179] To the Vettuvar belong the making of salt and earth-work.[180] To the Pulluvan (the medicine-man) and the Valluvan, labour for hire.

Then the Kunnuvazhikal (dwellers on the hills) are said to be 16 races. The Puliyar (to these the Kurumbiyatiri gave these rights: rule over the hill portion, holding land by clearing it, kettipachal, hunting, war and hired service).[181] [63] In the hills the Paniyanmar (training in arms), the Paniyar, Kadar, Kattuvar, Kurichiya Panikkar, Mavilavar, Karimbalar, Tuluvar and Kuluvar: rule of the forest, hunting, and work for wages in grain. The Irayavan, Eravalan, Ten Kurumbar (honey Kurumbar), Malayar, Kalladimar (the eravakkali dance, dancing in costume, laying demons), Alar, Peralar, Ullalar, Ullavar, Malayalar and Kurumbar: to raise many kinds of seed.[182] The Muttoran (hunting, making nets, plaiting the uri, a hanging net for pots). The Kuravan: to draw out poison, snake-charming, sleight-of-hand tricks, reading the hand, eating the flesh of crows, and making grass mats. The Parayan (the twelve clans born of the Paraya woman; Vayillakunnilappan is their tutelary deity): to make umbrellas and winnowing-fans, to work odi sorcery and enchantment, and to eat the flesh of the cow.[183] Those who have risen among the Cherumar are the Irular or Erulan and the Kanakkar; last of all the Pulayar too: to make mats; and the Nayadis: to kill dogs and eat them.[184][185]

In this way, for the 72 castes, the rules between one and another of bathing after touch and bathing after approach, the order, custom, law and standing, the sprinkling and the bathing, pollution and purification, the giving and changing of cloths, the day rites and the month rites: all this was laid down by Sankaracharyar.[186] At a time when the Brahmans of the 64 gramams (Brahman settlements), and the other villages, gramams, swarupams and the various castes were gathered in full, on the day after the Mahamakham that fell in the month of Kumbham when Jupiter enters Cancer, on the sandbank of the Perar river at Tirunavayi, he summoned the Perumal who as great king held and ruled the 17 nadus (districts) of the Malayalam country, and the Nambis, the Madambis, the Smartas and many other lords, [64] and made it known to them.[187] Know that this was the all-knowing Sankaracharyar.

“God makes no distinction of caste in anyone. Among foreigners no caste has the bath after approach; they all go as of one colour. That will not do in this land of works; here the land can be made pure only by works. In the kingdoms that are lands of knowledge all may walk together. In the land of works salvation can be won by works, and therefore it has been ordained and settled in this way. For those who put a hindrance in its way, poverty, grievous sickness, distress and sorrow of mind will never end. Therefore it must not be departed from.”[188] So it was decreed from the assembly in which the 64 gramams, Sankaracharyar, the kings, many holy persons and the great body of the people were met.

7. How Cheraman Perumal divided Kerala and gave it away

While Cheraman Perumal was thus reigning at his ease, he reflected in his royal mind: “It was to the Brahmans that Parasurama gave this land with the pouring of water. Now that I have enjoyed it for so long, what means is there of making amends?”[189] When he had so reflected, many sastris submitted to him that in the six sastras and the 3 Vedas no expiation[190] was to be found, and that only in the fourth Veda was there a release from it.[191]

[65] At that time Cheraman Perumal, listening to “a woman's word”, resolved that Padamala Nayar, the minister in charge of his bodyguard, must be seized and punished. The cause was this: the Perumal's wife desired that minister, and though she spoke words of love to him she could not bring him to consent. In her anger she swore, “I will have you cooked in boiling oil,” and by cunning she won the Perumal over. When he had been taken and set in the river called Azhiyar and they were about to punish him, Padamala Nayar said, “Only when my pay has been given me may I be killed,” and Cheraman Perumal commanded, “Pay him his allowance in full.”[192] They cut off the lower end of Padamala Nayar's cloth and held it out in the river, the lap of the cloth was held up, the days and the dues were reckoned up, and his allowance was paid in full. The measuring out of the rice was done as well, and from that day the river Azhiyar had the name Ariyar (rice river).[193]

As they were about to punish him, the gods let down a heavenly car from the world of heaven and said, “Mount the car.” Padamala Nayar said to the Ten Thousand, “Carry on my office of the bodyguard.”[194] As he mounted the car and was going, the Perumal entreated him, “What way of salvation is there for me?” He answered: “At the place of pilgrimage, at Hajj Chaturapuram, there is a Jonakan named Veda Azhiyar. If you go and see him he will confirm you in the fourth Veda and show you the sign. For that, have the men of this Veda fit out an olamari ship, [66] have it brought to shore at Tiruvanchazhimukham, embrace their faith and go with them on the pilgrimage, and you will gain half of salvation.”[195][196] Having said this, Padamala Nayar entered heaven.

After that, at the time when the Brahmans and the Perumal were bathing in the great tirtha (sacred bathing-place) on the day of the Mahamakham, he fell out with the Vedic Brahmans over the Veda, and made up his mind that he must go on the pilgrimage with the Bauddhas. The lord called Cheraman Perumal, having come to old age, decreed that his kingdom should be shared out among the people who were dear to him.

Between Kanyakumari and Gokarnam, between Kannetti and Putupattanam: on the south the bar of Changalapuram, on the north the bar of Putupattanam, on the east the pass-gates of the 18 ghats, on the west the 18 river-mouths upon the sea; the north-west corner the corner of Agni, the north-east corner the corner of Isana, the south-east corner the Vadapurayi corner, the south-west corner the Chempurayi corner.[197] Within these lies Cheraman's country, the land of Parasurama: a country 160 katam (leagues, of about 5 miles each) in length, with 4,448 consecrated shrines of gods, 108 temples of Durga, 360 shrines of bhutas (spirits), 1,008 nalpattiradi (fencing grounds), 64 gramams, 96 towns, 18 fortified places and 17 nadus: Tulunadu, Kolattunadu, Polanadu, Kurumbanadu, Puravazhinadu, Eranadu, Parappunadu, Valluvanadu, Ravananadu, Vettattunadu, Tirumanasserinadu, Perimpadappunadu, Nedunganadu, Venganadu, Muringanadu, Onanadu and Venadu. The 5 adjoining nadus are said to be Pandi, Kongu, Tulu, Vayanadu [67] and Punnadu.[198] It is reported that, not counting Kerala, Konkanam and Kodagu, there are 56 kingdoms.[199]

In the country of Cheraman thus described he appointed Udayavarman Kolattiri to be the Northern Perumal, a crowned king and overlord of Kerala.[200] He ordained that 350,000 Nayars of nine hundred and forty-four illams (houses) should, on the right side of the fort of Valarbhattam, with backs held straight, gird on the dagger, serve him as retainers and wait upon him. He gave him also the Perumal's kattaram (dagger) and commanded that he should uphold the share of the Lord of Ventrikkovil. He sent for the Nayaka Nambutirippadu of the Puliyappadambu house at Perinchellur,[201] set him to administer the god's share, had the god's rice put upon the king and had the royal consecration performed. To the two Nambiyars called Chuzhanna Kammal, or Chuzhali, and Nervetta Kammal, the madambis (barons) of the Kola swarupam, he gave the rank of idavazhcha (subordinate chiefship) over a country 12 katam long, and 1,200 Nayars each. He blessed Udayavarman and declared, “If I come back, the junior rank; if I do not come back, the Cheraman title and the place of overlord.”[202] He also ordained, “In this way rule henceforward for 100 years; after that the right to rule is the strong man's.”

In the south, to the Venadadikal, who is the swarupam of Kulasekhara, he granted 350,000 Nayars, to gird on the dagger and serve at the Omana Putiya Kovilakam on the right side of the fort of Kalkkulam, together with the koyma (authority) over the country, joining Onanadu to Venadu.[203] [68] He said to him, “Stand as a support to the Kola swarupam and spend your wealth for it,” and appointed him to reign in the Kuvala kingdom. To this day the two swarupams observe death-pollution for each other.[204] He gave him much property and charged him also to guard the Chitrakutam.

Then to the Surya Kshatriya he granted a country of 52 katam, a great body of men, 18 madambis and 42 ministers, and gave the name Perimpadappu.[205] Know that among the ministers Balyattachan is the foremost; they are in no way wanting in war.[206]

His younger brother, the lord Kavisimhamarera, he appointed to protect Tulunadu, and gave him the place of overlord north of the Perimpuzha. He also ordained that the Parambar, the Bangar of Nandavar; the Ajalar; the Savittar, the Chautar of Mudubidri; and the Samantareru, the Samantar of Mulukki, these 4 lords, should be the support of Kavisimhamarera.[207]

Polanadu, the choicest of countries, with the 10,000 Nayars born with the birth of men, with its 3 kuttams (assemblies), its 72 taras (Nayar village units) and its five bodies of escort, a country of three katam, he gave to the Poralatiri king.[208] He commanded him to reside in the Mallur kovilakam and to keep up the 18 usages. These are: the hide, the leg, the shaft, the black blanket, the duel (ankam), the service holding (virutti), the toll, ezha and kozha (levies), the elephant, the sword, the hero's chain, the badge of honour, music, the appointed firing of guns, the frontlet (of the state elephant), the war-seat, the war-house, the flying kuttu, the sprinkling in front and the chiruta cry: such are the 18 usages of Polanadu.[209][210]

[69] Next, to the Kurumbiyatiri king he gave a country of 36 katam and the 30,000 Nayars born with the birth of gods, and 1,200 taras for them.[211] Then to the king of Kollam he gave Kollam, a country of three katam, to rule. To the king of Pantalam he gave a country five katam long in the midst, where Venadu and Onanadu meet. He appointed also the Parappu swarupam, the Vettattu swarupam, the Cherayi swarupam of Kayankulam and others.[212]

Last of all, in order that the Mahamakham festival might be kept and carried on, he granted to the Valluvakkonatiri king the sandbank of Tirunavayi, a country and 10,000 Nayars. He pronounced it the Arangattu (Arngottur) swarupam, and to protect the swarupam he appointed the Bhagavati of Tirumanamkunnu, in the Chovvaram party, as the tutelary deity of the house.[213] In this way he gave the 17 nadus to 18 kings and laid down the 18 usages.

Panniyur and Chovaram, the 2 parties, and the Paravu party, making three; Bhatta, Prabhakara and Vyakarana; in these three parties, six apiece, he also appointed for them 18 sanghams. Their names are: in the Bhatta party, Nenmini, Chovaram, Attichunda, Natti, these six; in the Prabhakara party, Palavakka, Viti, Vellam, Tittuchazhi, these six; in the Vyakarana party, Tattavezham and Vallukandu, these six; thus 18 sanghams.[214] So the king called Cheraman Perumal set up each man to rule in his own nadu.

Hearing that the Perumal had finished sharing out the kingdom and was about to set out on the pilgrimage to Mecca, [70] the Punturakkon (the two Eradis; it is told, however, that Manichan had died fighting against Krishna Rayar) and Mangattu Unnikkumara Menon went (to the Chitrakutam at Trikkariyur) to see the Perumal.[215] He considered, “The kingdom has already been shared out and given to the people who were dear to me; what is to be done now?” and then the Perumal said, “There is still the desam (locality) where the cock crows, and the thorn jungle; that I will give you (you did not come a little sooner).”[216] When they had decided that this was enough, Cheraman Perumal, with the Valluvakkonatiri standing by, poured water from a golden conch and gave them what was left, Kozhikode and Chullikkadu and a road three kol wide by the elephant-rod (together with the Jonakar, the Kadi and the rest, to sail ships to Mecca, and the charge of guarding the Mamangam festival), and gave the sword, pouring the water over the sword (its point).[217]

He gave the order, “Dying and killing, bring the land under you,” and the leave, “Throughout this hill country hold the place of overlord as I myself have done.”[218][219] Then the Punturakkonatiri king received it with both hands full, and the water that ran over was drunk first of all by Unnikkumara Nambiyar of Toduvikkalam. At that moment the Perumal with his own royal tongue named him Mangattarayarachan Menon, and named the junior of the Kunnalakonatiri the Nambiyatiri Tirumulpadu.[220]

That day he gave him also such royal honours as these: the paramadhani (state carpet), the patani and the pallimaradi; to have the white umbrella of victory held over him, to have the silver trumpet sounded, [71] to be fanned with the peacock fan and the white yak-tail; to punish thieves and the wicked, to gladden cows and Brahmans, to protect women and children; the governance of the country and the 18 usages; the standing lamp, the torch, the pearl umbrella, the green fan-standard, the matchless flag and the firing of guns on the march. And he declared, “Setting up storehouse and port, fetters, stocks and stake, protecting tali (temple-council seat) and sanketam (temple sanctuary land), with all the adornments of royalty, as sole lord under one umbrella, hold and rule over the sea-girt earth from Kumari to Gokarnam.”[221] That they might reign with the portion of a lord of men, binding six and eight circles, he gave the sword to Manichan and the water to Vikraman.[222]

When he saw this the Valluvakkonatiri submitted to Cheraman Perumal, “It was with the words ‘Cut and conquer’ that you gave the sword. Now you must appoint some protection for me.” The Perumal said, “So be it; stand and ward off,” gave the Valluvakkonatiri a shield, and gave three cuts upon the shield. Because he went away having given the sword to conquer with and the shield to ward off and protect with, to this day no war can be waged upon the Valluvakkonatiri. He also said, “Join battle with the kings other than the Venadadikal and the Kolattiri,” and ordained the name of the Nediyirippu (Nidivirippu) swarupam.[223]

Having thus set up 18 kings in the 17 nadus, he gave a desam each to such men as the Nambi, the Nambidi, the Nambutiri and the Nambiyatiri, [72] and appointed them to be madambis under one swarupam or another. To the Vellalar also he granted the rank of idavazhcha in many nadus, and the titles Vazhunnor, Kartta, Kambammikikal, Nayar, Menon, Pilla and Panikkar.

He charged 4 men to see that the law and standing ordained in the 17 nadus were carried on without decay, and that the customs and usages were entered in the pattola (record roll): 1. in the Trippa swarupam of Venadu, the Pandarappilla of the Omana Putiya Kovil at Kalkkulam; 2. in Perimpadappu, Valiyattu Menon; 3. in Eranadu, at Nedivirippu, Mangatturayaracha Menon; 4. in the Kolattiri swarupam, Putisseri Nambiyar, the officer of the country and accountant. Because lordship was conferred on Mangattachan besides, the other 3 must pay their respects to the Menon.[224]

On the day of the auspicious Puyam in the month of Makaram when Jupiter is in Cancer, these four keepers of the pattola sat down together in one place on the sandbank of Tirunavayi.[225] The 4 pattolas were unrolled, the customs, order and usages current between Kanyakumari and Gokarnam were set forth, and the Brahmans, madambis, subjects and lords were informed of them; and he commanded that a Mahamakham festival, an ornament to all the subjects of the 17 nadus, should be held as a service under the royal umbrella of the Valluvakkon. He also declared that the madambis of the seventeen nadus, holding their lands, should at every succession bring their bundles and purses [73] to the fort of Valarbhattam for the royal consecration, and should behold the royal feet of the Kolattiri, the Northern Perumal, and pay him homage.

Having ordained all this, he went from the sandbank of Tirunavayi to Tirupanchakkalam, had the men of the Veda brought ashore from their ship, and proceeded to the palace of Kodungallur in order to set out on the pilgrimage.[226] Together with the men of the Veda Cheraman Perumal went on board the ship and departed for Mecca. The Kali reckoning is “Cheraman desaprapyah” (Cheraman reached the other land). The Christian year 355.[227][228]

Hear also the old tradition that the Mappilas tell.[229] Cheraman Perumal went secretly on board a ship at the port of Kodungallur, lay for a day in the roads of Kollam by Koyilandi, came the next day to Dharmapattanam and stayed 3 days, entrusted the guarding of the kovilakam of Dharmapattanam to the Tamutiri, and went on board and sailed away.[230] After that the shipmen and others of Kodungallur went out and fought hard with the men of the ship in which the Perumal had embarked, but could not take it, and he landed at the port called Sehar Mukalha.[231] At that time the Prophet Muhammad was living in the land called Vijiddha.[232] He went there, they met each other, he embraced the faith and took the name of Tajuddin. He married Rajiyatt, the sister of Malik Habib Dinar, a king in Arabia, and stayed 5 years.

After that the said king, his fifteen children and the Perumal came to the land of Sehar [74] Mukalha, built a spacious house and a mosque, and lived there in comfort. While he was preparing and making ready for the voyage, in order to come to the Malayalam country and establish the Din (the faith), he was seized with an ague and brought low. He then sent off the said king with his sons, bearing letters to the kings of the Malayalam country, and after that Tajuddin died and was buried in the very mosque that he had built.[233]

That king took the Perumal's seal and letters, went on board 2 ships with his wife, children and the rest, and sailed. One ship came to the roads of Madura, and the fourth son, Takayuddin, and others landed, built a mosque and so on, and settled there. The other ship came to Kodungallur; with the king's consent he had a mosque built there, and Muhammad stayed as Kadi. The 3rd was the mosque at Kollam near Koyilandi, with Asan as Kadi; 4. the mosque of Madayi, Abidurahaman Kadi; 5. the mosque of Vakkanur, Ibrahim Kadi; 6. the mosque at Maikkalam, Mussu Kadi; 7. at Kanjarottu, Malik Kadi; 8. the mosque at Siravupattanam, Shihabuddin Kadi; 9. the mosque of Dharmapattanam, Usain Kadi; 10. the mosque at Pantalani, Saiduddin Kadi; 11. at Chaliyam, Sainuddin Kadi. In this way he built 11 mosques, laying in each one a block of the dark stone brought from Arabia.[234] The king and the others came and established the Din everywhere in the Malayalam country, and while he was living there in comfort he fell sick [75] and died, and was buried in the mosque of Kodungallur itself. When he met the Perumal the Prophet was 57 years old.[235]

Part 3. The Age of the Tamburans

1. How the Tamutiri subdued Polanadu

Among the kings of the Malayalam country the Kunnalakonatiri king in particular is called lord of the hill and of the waves, and this is said because he holds back the enemies who come by way of the hills and by way of the sea. The Kunnalakonatiri considered by what means he might make the people of Polanadu also his own. Seated in the gatehouse at Panniyankara, he wrote and sent for the lesser lords of the Charava party and the Putukkotta party, and said, “You must be our allies (and must stand as our support).” They answered, “So be it,” and clasping hands they made a compact.[1]

In the Charava party: first the Vettamudaya Kovilpadu (lord of 5,000 Nayars); the Nambidi of Payyanadu, 5,000 Nayars; the Nambidi of Mangattu, 12 Nayars; Mukkudakkadu, in its 3 branches together, 500 Nayars (5,000); the Eastern Nambidi of Periyandamukku, 1,000 Nayars. All this is what belongs to the Charava party west of Kuttakkadavu.[2]

Now in the Putukkotta party: the chief of them, the Tirumalasseri Nambutirippadu, 3,000 Nayars; the Nambidi of Maniyur, [76] 100; the Kozhikkolli Nayar, 300; the Western Nambidi of Periyandamukku, 500; the Kottummal Padanayakan, 300; the Irikkalikkal Adhikaran, 300. All this is what belongs to the Putukkotta party west of Kuttakkadavu.

Above Nedunganadu, in the southern party: the Karttavu, 100 Nayars; Karakkattu Mutta Nayar, 1,000; Vittiyakkattu Padanayar, 300; Vittikkattu Tekka Nayar, 100. This is the southern party. East of Kuttakkadavu, above Nedunganadu, in the northern party: the Karttavu, 100; Karimbuzha Ilambilasseri Nayar, 300; Kannanur Padanayar, 500; Nedunganattu Padanayar, 300; the Vadakkan Nayar of the southern party, 300; Muriyalattu Nayar, 300; Cherangattu Kulappalli Nayar, 300; Mulanja Padanayar, 300; Mankara, 500; Venmannur Vellottu Adhikaran, 100; Kuzhal Kunnattu Puliyakkottu Mutta Nayar, 500; Kongasseri Nayar, 100; Alipparambil Menon, 100; the Mele Talaparkku and the Kelanallur Talaparkku together, 500; with that included, Kutirapattattu Nayar, 5,004; the Nambidi of Venganadu, 1,000; Machuttiraman Ulladar, 1,000; in the Vadakare party, Pilasseri Nayar, 50.[3]

Such were the lesser lords, the madambis and the men who came together that day. Those of the Charava party were put to service under the royal umbrella of the Tamutiri,[4] and those of the Putukkotta party to service under the royal umbrella of the junior prince (of Eranadu), the Nambiyatiri Tirumulpadu; and the forces were brought up. Seated at Panniyankara, the Kunnalakonatiri [77] made war upon the Poralatiri king for four times twelve years. Polanadu, a country three katam long, with 72 taras and 10,000 Nayars, and among them 3 kuttams, 32 taravadu families and 5 bodies of escort (children born of one mother, one hired service, rice from one cauldron, service under one umbrella): this was what belonged to the Poralatiri king.[5]

When the Kunnalakonatiri had fought against them and, being unable to prevail, had drawn off, he went to Sri Porkkolli and served the Bhagavati for 6 months. When she appeared to him he prayed, “Your Holiness must come with me to my kingdom, so that I may conquer wherever I go.” She granted the boon, “So be it.” He saw her vanish upon the door, and concluding that the constant presence of the Bhagavati was in the door itself, he carried the door away with him.

When he had come back here the Manavikramans and the Vettamudaya Kovil took counsel together and resolved that the whole Ten Thousand of the escort must be won over.[6] They sent Unnikkumara Menon and Parachankara Nambi with word to meet and speak with the men of the escort. The two of them went, met the chief men and spoke with them, and sent back word that the place appointed was the Perimpilakkal (“at the great jack tree”), where Ganapati is ever present. In the afternoon, when the Manavikramans and the Brahmans went to the Veran jack tree, they were glad to see the chief men of the escort, [78] and each side told the other everything that had happened in times past. When these said, “We will not stand against you in your endeavour to drive out the Poralatiri,” the compact was made with them: “Our rank and your rank shall be made alike.”

Then they brought in the Menokki, who was the favourite and the minister of the Poralatiri, and took counsel with him. “If you will dislodge the Poralatiri without fighting, send him out of that country and put Polanadu into our hands, then for as long as this kingdom is ours we will add to your dignities without stint.” When, on that compact, he had told them how the driving out should be done, a compact was made with the Menokki: “We will make you a junior prince; as a second line, with the rule of Eranadu, we will give you half the koyma, with land and people.”[7]

The four ministers (1. the Achan, 2. the Elayathu, 3. the Panikkar and 4. Para Nambi) took counsel together and saw Nalakattuttamma (the Chalappurattamma, who was the Nayaki, the lady).[8] They made a compact with her: “(Stand as an ally on our side and) contrive some means (give some counsel) by which the fort may be taken, and we will give you 4 elephants and 40,000 panam. Nor is that all: if the gate of the fort is opened to us, we will give you the rank of Amma in 4 houses and install you as the fourth line.” She agreed and went.[9]

After that the Poralatiri [79] sent the princes and princesses who were his heirs to Kolattunadu to see his elder brother, and stayed himself in the fort of Polur. Word of this was written to Kozhikode, and the Manavikramans and all the rest bestirred themselves. At daybreak, when the Poralatiri had been anointed with oil and had gone to Marakkulangara, the gate of the fort was opened; the Nediyirippu entered the fort, took his seat there and had three mortars fired. Hearing the shots, the Poralatiri king said only, “Have they betrayed me?” and without finishing his bath he went to the Koladi (Kolodi) kovil. There he finished his bath, took his gruel and his meal, sent for the people of Kizhalur and Kurumbattur, and said:

“Let Polur and Cherupatta bear no males (let none be born); and if males are born, let them not carry on what is fitting. In our country there must be no house built above a house, no twisted bangle, no wearing of the hero's silk, no ploughing with yoked buffaloes and no milking of them. You must stand as my support (be my support). So long as protection and punishment in the country lose nothing of their vigour (do not fail), no decline or downfall shall come to you, and the foremost place before eye and hand, and the right to it, shall be yours in the country.”[10] Thus the Poralatiri king spoke and blessed them, and they answered that so it should be. [80] It is told that the Nayars of Kizhalur marched as his escort, saw him across Turasseri, made their obeisance and came back. So he crossed Turasseri and went his way.

As he was going to his bath, he learned that the Thousand Nayars had surrounded the fort, and went quickly inside the fort and called the Menokki and the Nayaki of Chalappuram before him. “That what you two told me earlier is true, I am now fully satisfied. Consider and tell me whether I ought to die or to quit the kingdom.” They reckoned, “If the king dies fighting we shall have to die with him,” and submitted, “To fight with the Manavikramans and conquer them is a hard matter; it is better therefore to quit the kingdom.” He said, “You must gather our people and have them stand and fight. Meanwhile I shall change my dress and go.” This was done, and the Poralatiri left the fort and went away.[11]

The Poralatiri, cast out of his kingdom and beaten in war, set out and went to the Kolattiri, in whose swarupam he had put his trust. The Kolattiri, for the upkeep of his high rank, gave him a country of three katam and 3,000 Nayars, and settled him there with the name of Nattadi (Adiyodi). That is the line of the Tamburan of Kaduttanadu.[12] The Kolattiri gave it with the consent of the Kurumbiyatiri king. To the Poralatiri king were given [81] Kaduttanadu, a country three katam long, the Vazhunnor of the Putiya Kovilakam, Ilankulam Kuruppu, Tottattil Nambiyar, Narangoli Nambiyar, Porkkattusseri Nambiyar, Chembatta Kuruppu, 3,000 Nayars, and the Bhagavati of Kavil; such was the settlement.[13]

When the country had thus been brought under him, the Valiya Tamburan (senior lord) of the Tamutiri's house made the Menokki ruler in Eranadu, assigned him half the koyma and 5,000 Nayars, and said, “You are to carry on the koyma of the Poralatiri.” He took by the hand the Kizhinniyar of the Prabhakara party (the Kizhnir Menokki?) and commanded, “Protect the god and the temple property in the 4 mukkalpattam temples, Ollur, Polur, Talakollattur and Chelannur; take care of the illams and houses connected with the Kizhinniyar; and for the rest carry on as an idavazhcha line, to the extent of a half in each thing”; and with his royal tongue he pronounced him “Menon of Eranadu”.[14] The Kunnalakonatiri king granted the Nayakiyar positions of rule and the name “Talachennor of Kozhikode”, and gave sword and cloth.[15]

After this the people of Vadakkumpuram would not be reconciled, but turned and stood in opposition. When they said, “If this koyma is exercised in the country, will not the order and custom and the standing in law of our women and children be impaired?” he ordained that, so that the koyma might be exercised without wrong being done in the country, it should be the god of the Tali who held it; he brought the god down before them and seated him in the [82] kovil, appointed the Talachennor, and commanded, “Out of what comes in for wrongs done in the country, provide the offering of ghee to the god of the Tali without fail.”[16][17] He made this known to the people and appointed elders.

After that the 10,000 and the king came to terms with each other. He assigned to each of them a rank, a dignity and a right. Making them his own retainers, he fixed their service too, and put them to service under the umbrella of the Achan and the Elayathu. He brought the body together under the Veran jack tree and seated the assembly. The Achan and the Elayathu went to the head of the Nizhal and set up the treasury of the Nizhal, made the Bhagavati of Tiruvalayanadu the tutelary deity of the Nizhal, and declared the rank and dignity of the king and of the people.[18] He sent for the Nayars of the fort, called the assembly by beat of drum, had Para Nambi set up the royal board, and ordered that the people be given day and measure for their expenses.[19] So that the higher custom and the lower custom might be known, Mangattachan wrote them down and made a pattola of them, and wrote down also the old precedents for the people to cite.

In this way the people and the ruling house were joined together; and while the 10,000, the 3,000, the 30,000, the Akattuttu parisha and the people of Payyanattinkara were together governing the country, the idavazhcha (the lesser chiefship) and the naduvazhcha (the rule of the nadu) fell out with each other. The people of Vadakkumpuram, who took the side of the idavazhcha line, and the people of Kizhakkumpuram, who took the side of the naduvazhcha line, made ready to cut each other down: they tied on the war-cloth, smeared themselves with sandal and took up their weapons.[20] When the people of Vadakkumpuram [83] came to force their way into the Tamutiri's kovilakam and die there, the people of Kizhakkumpuram also took up their weapons and posted themselves at the gate of the kovilakam.

Mangattachan saw this, and judging that “these men will hack each other to death and ruin the swarupam,” he went before them, brought them to understand the matter, cleared up the quarrel and made the people clasp one another's hands. “Make your obeisance and withdraw,” Mangattachan said; “then the custom of hired service will stand for you for ever”; and he had the people do service accordingly in the presence of the king. After that he began to hold games with the people on many grounds, and the oleri race as well.[21]

Then he installed in rule the Amma who was Tamburatti in the Ayambadi kovilakam, established 5 lines of rule and 5 kovilakams, and enshrined the tutelary deities.[22] In the same way to the idavazhcha line he gave sword and cloth, saying, “Carry on as 5 lines of rule,” and granted, “Have the palanquin, the Pallichan bearers and the women attendants, the sprinkling in front, the chiruta cry and the dignity of an escort.” So granted the Kunninnu Konatiri.[23]

2. The building of the city of Kozhikode

After that he finished building a fort at Velapuram in Kozhikode, brought the storehouses and the harbours under his control, and had the alavattam (peacock-feather fan) and the venchamaram (white yak-tail whisk) waved before him.[24] A Chetti[25] who lived on the eastern [84] sea-coast boarded a ship, sailed to Mecca[26] and traded there. Because he loaded on more gold than the ship could hold, the ship was on the point of sinking; so when he came abreast of the harbour of Kozhikode he put in to shore, took some gold in a box, laid it as a presentation-offering before the Tamutiri and reported what had happened. Hearing it, the king said, “Keep the gold here yourself.” The Chetti then built a chamber of granite in the Tamutiri's kovilakam (palace). Gifts were given in plenty and the vault was taken in charge, and he kept on board only as much as he saw the ship could carry. All the rest of the gold he brought and laid before the king, declared its amount, and at an auspicious hour placed that wealth in the stone vault and closed it; then he took his leave, boarded his ship and went away.

When a little time had passed he came back to take away the wealth he had deposited, laid a presentation-offering before the king and reported the matter. Then he opened the stone vault, took out the wealth that had been placed there, declared its amount in the royal presence, divided it in two and said, “One share is for the king and one share for me.” The king replied, “Take your wealth away yourself.” Hearing this, the Chetti was convinced that there had never been a king and a swarupam (ruling house) as honest as this, and he had Mangattachan[27] told, “You must grant me leave to trade from this harbour.” [85] Mangattachan reported it accordingly; it pleased the royal mind, and the king said, “So be it.”

Then master-builders were summoned to build a city on the seashore. They marked off the ground from the kovilakam, stretched the cord and measured, chose the site and drove in the pegs, and at an auspicious hour laid the stones and built, set up posts and built a street. The Chetti lived there doing works of charity and piety, and began shipping and trade; his name was Ambaresan. What he built in the kovilakam is to this day called Ambaresan-kettu (Ambaresan's building). The place where the building of the city began is Chetti-teru (the Chetti street). Many others built streets and began to trade, and the Marakkars of the harbour[28] too sent ships to Mecca; and many say that in shipping, in the keeping of accounts, in income and expenditure, in justice and fines, and in the profits of trade there is no other country, city or government in the world like this one.[29]

For the building of the city the Uralis were the chief workmen. Formerly they kept and milked cattle for the Lord of Trichammaram[30] and supplied milk and ghee; hence the tradition that they are Gopalas (cowherds). Because the Kolattiri king hated them they could not remain there; they withdrew from that country, came to the Parappu kovilakam and waited on the king, and had taken up various kinds of work to earn their daily living when the building of the city of Kozhikode began. They were then sent for to cut and clear the thorn-scrub on the seashore. While they were thus going about at work for a long time, Mangattachan, by the command of the Kunnalakonatiri, [86] had them cut down and kill the 60 Nambis who were the trustees (uralar) of the Tali,[31] and drag the bodies away and dispose of them. For this he gave them the janmam (hereditary proprietary right), the family estates and the trusteeship of the Tali that had belonged to those men. The king has also kept them in paid military service among the Ten Thousand.

3. The defeat of the Valluvakkonatiri

By the direction of the sannyasi called the Kolkunnattu Sivangal (Sivayogi; Sivamayan), he performed rites and made gifts at the Tali, and with the blessing of the Brahmans protected the Tali and its sanketam (temple sanctuary land). He sent ships to Mecca. He resolved to take his stand on the sandbank of Tirunavayi and to protect and conduct the Mahamakham festival.[32] (He cut down and conquered the Arangottu swarupam,[33] and brought it under the Nediyirippu swarupam and ruled it.) He removed the Valluvakkonatiri king, upheld truth and justice, subdued 17 nadus (districts) and brought the gates of 18 forts under him. Thus stands the Neduvirippu swarupam.

On the island of Maskiyattu[34] two sons were born. When these sons of one father fell out, their father said to the elder: “You two will strive against each other, and after my time the other will kill you. Therefore you must not both remain here. Go to some other island [87] and spend your days there; that is the best thing for you. For that I will give you gold enough to live on.” So saying he gave him as much wealth as a ship would hold and sent him away. He went to many kingdoms, saw the kings who ruled in each, and laid presentation-offerings before them. And what did he offer? He put gold in a box, covered it over with pickle, closed it, and set it down saying that it was pickle. The reason for doing so was this: he offered it in that way to test each of them, so as to tell the honest from the dishonest and know whom to trust (so that he might settle where there was honesty). The kings, in their greed, did not tell the truth of it. Then, when he saw Punturakkon and laid it before him, the king said: “Look, this has been changed on you by mistake; this is not pickle, it is gold.” When he said this, the man was convinced that here was one who could be trusted. This is how the Koya (Kosa) of Kozhikode came.[35]

One day, when the Vilvamangalattu Sivangal (Sivamayan) was travelling from the north towards Rameswaram,[36] Punturakkon told him of his affairs at the Tali of Kozhikode, and the Sivangal said: “To remove the misfortune that is coming upon this place and this swarupam, you must have gifts, works of piety and services to the gods performed.” When he asked, “How is that to be done?” and “What things are needed?”, [88] the Sivangal said: “The gift is this. In this temple, every year in the month of Tulam, for 7 days beginning with Revati,[37] give food (a feast) to all the people who come; tie up 101 panam[38] each for one hundred and one Smartas[39] and give them away; and have performed the rites that are proper to kings, namely tulabharam, hiranyagarbham,[40] maha-mrityunjayam,[41] the flying kuttu, kudiyattam[42] and the reading of the Bharatam,[43] and also a great Ganapati homam (fire-offering) and Bhagavati seva (worship of the Goddess). Take in hand and protect what you yourself ought to maintain, what has been brought under you here, and the temples that are falling down for want of repair; and duly appoint the worship, offerings and so on in each place ….”[44]

Then he submitted, “There is no money for that.” On hearing this the Sivangal said: “That does not matter at all. If you borrow and do it, wealth will come to you when you least expect it; afterwards write up the accounts and spend it. Keep up the daily gifts, the feeding of people to the full and the gilding with gold, and fortune (Sri) will stay. If the pride of fortune should grow, you may have ‘Sri’ proclaimed, and may have water sprinkled before you, in a dish of leavings.”[45] Thus the great sannyasi laid down customs for the swarupam and blessed him. From that time he began to do so in faith.

The man who had come at that time (the Jonakan)[46] gave much gold and, trusting this swarupam, built a house and lived there. Now the Mahamakham falls, as is known, in the month of Kumbham in the year when Jupiter is in Karkatakam;[47] [89] on that day there is the sacred bathing in the Perar at Tirunavayi, and there the kings of the Chovaram faction[48] in this Kerala have their place of state (nilapadu) and their dignities. The Koya set out to see it, having informed the king, saw the Mahamakham and came back. “How was it?” the king asked. “At this Mahamakham, besides the flowing of the divine waters, what splendours are there?” The Koya not only told him of all the splendours there; he submitted: “All those places ought by right to belong to our swarupam.” The great king said, “There is nothing we can do about that.” Hearing this he said, “If this dignity is wanted here, your servant will seize it and give it to you.” Punturakkon, hearing this, said, “Then I will have you stand on my right side.”

When he heard that, he by sea and the others by land marched south with an army and conquered, subduing one nadu, town, gramam (Brahman settlement) and temple after another, and by the time a cycle of Jupiter was complete he had reached Tirunavayi (and subdued those places too). There, in his magnificence, he provided kambavedi (fireworks) and kalpala (ship-sailing?),[49] a spectacle such as no one had ever seen before, and set aside funds so that it should never fall short. The king called him by the name “Koya of Kozhikode”, gave him many dignities and placed him on his right side. In the same way, [90] to show that he has no rival, he has the Santaswami stand beside him holding conch and umbrella.[50]

At that time the dignity that had belonged to the Chovaram faction passed under the Panniyur faction. Know that because of that affront, from that day to this, by the command of the Bhagavati of Tirumanamkunnu, the warriors (chekavar) of the Angottur (Arangottu) swarupam (fight in single combat and) die.[51] On that day 400 less ten palanquins and 1,200 (long) umbrellas were given over; leaving the overlordship of the Angottur swarupam, they came under the Nediyirippu swarupam. From that day the one side had the going about by night and the other the going about by day.[52] He began to seize and subdue one nadu and town after another. From then on there was no enemy left to stand against this swarupam.

For the administration of Vellappanattukara, Tarakkal Ittunnirama Vari was made Chunnakkadu Talachennor and given sword and cloth,[53] as master of 1,000 Nayars. Then a royal minister named Chulliyil Sankara Nambi seized Malappuram, three katam (leagues of about 5 miles) of the Valluvakkonatiri's country; therefore “eye, face and wick” (kannum mukavum tiriyum) were given him for that office.[54] That is the reason for the name Malappurattu Para Nambi.[55]

4. The greatness of Kozhikode

It is the settled doctrine in the Malayalam country that the Kunnalakonatiri king is the Maharaja. From that time on, with the exception of the Venadu Adikal in the south [91] and the Kolattiri king in the north, he would go from time to time and give battle to the other kings, until the middle of Edavam.[56] When the middle of Edavam is past, they assemble under the Veran jack-tree, a pavilion is appointed, the men are given their expenses, and the Achan and the Elayathu go in front; and when the army gathers, he gives battle only after writing to the Chovaram faction. The reason for the saying “Mangattachan makes no treacherous war” is that he gives notice: “Whether it holds together and goes forward, or ends in a fall, we shall see.”[57] Know that the Nediyirippu swarupam has conquered and grown great by honesty.

The kings of foreign countries, the Punnadan, the Mayisuran, the Mayilomban, the Chadakkaran, the Mukilan, the Mukkupariyan (the nose-cutter), the Ikkeriyan, the Mulukki, the Ammasi; the Kongan, the Pandiyan, the Paleriyan, the Setupati, the king of Kasi, the Parsavu (Padshah), the Chozha king, the Palicheyan, the Parintiris (French), the Inkiris (English), the Parangis (Portuguese), the Lanta (Dutch), the Dvipazhi, the Putukka king and others, with their armies, used in former times to come by sea and by land and attack.[58] They too, who came to subdue this land, were turned back, and not one enemy was left who could stand against it. In this land there are, as is known, a full 18 Vaishnava shrines and 96 towns. Of these the most renowned is Kozhikode; it has never known decline and never known calamity.

The reason is this. A Jonakan named Chemmangatta Auvvayi (Chengotta Avayan) served Sri Bhagavati and made a compact with her. He said, “Tomorrow morning let it be at Kantaparambu; if you do not see me there, [92] let it be in the town after noon; if you do not see me there either, let it be at Mukkadi at dusk; and if you do not see me there, stay in these three places until you see me.” Such was the compact he made; and that same day he went and died. Therefore the Bhagavati can never withdraw from that place.[59] From that day, as in Viratapuram,[60] everything that is wanted has been found in this city; goods of many kinds began to pour in and fill it, and people too began to fill it. As for how it came about that a Jonakan should see so glorious a Bhagavati: God himself ordained it so, because “only the Bauddhas have firmness of heart.”[61]

Further, when the Tamutiri dies, Mangattachan holds that office until the tiruvantali (the concluding funeral rite) is over. When the tiruvantali is over, many lords from village and gramam and elsewhere arrive in order to crown the princes in their order of seniority.[62] They bring the Mother of Tiruvalayanadu[63] in procession, pronounce “Brahma, Vishnu, Maheswara”, set out the golden lamp and the full measure of rice, adorn the floor and hang the canopy, and bring in the Pallimaradi (a royal emblem). He holds the Cheraman sword;[64] white cloth and a black blanket are spread on the throne; he puts on the hero's chain and ties on the royal head-band. The three-times-six, 18, sanghams of the two lines of Azhalur (Ayalur) and Sarkkara [93] come together and pronounce: “Exercise the koyma (authority) in the 16 nadus of Cheraman's country and in the dependent territories, protect cows, Brahmans and gods, gladden women and children, put an end to wrongdoing, and live and reign as Maharaja.” The Brahmans, Vettattu Kovil, Tinayancheri Elayathu and the Azhavancheri Tamprakkal[65] together place old rice upon his head; Dharmagunattu Panikkar girds on him the sword of state; he takes his seat in the portico of the treasury, and the lord-chief of the 5,000 Nayars makes obeisance and does service.

Then, when the service of the Ten Thousand is over, he sets out at an auspicious hour for Kozhikode. The 18 instruments are sounded; the pearl umbrella (the white umbrella of state) and the jewelled fan-standard are carried; he sits in the royal palanquin; the silver kalanchi and the golden kalanchi (betel vessels) are carried; shield-bearers with shields mounted in gold and silver clash them as an escort, a marching salute is fired, and with a multitude of musketeers he proceeds to Panniyankara and beholds the goddess Durga. The lord of the 5,000, the Talachennor of Kozhikode, the Kosa and the Katiyar[66] walk as escort beside the palanquin. Those of the Thirty-two among the Ten Thousand tie on girdle and head-cloth, and together with the men of the Ten Thousand go to Kallayi;[67] there, with the cry “The enemy in front!”, they perform the play of the three steps and make their submission, and walk as escort. A flowered cloth is spread, and he goes on foot and enters the Ayampadi kovilakam; he salutes the Mother, she places old rice upon his head, and having received her blessing he beholds the Lord of the Tali [94] and proceeds to Tiruvalayanadu and Parakkal, and has a salute fired.

Under the Veran jack-tree the assembly of the Ten Thousand meets and is feasted; the places of honour, the dignities and the privileges are recited, and the old traditions are recited; care is taken that there be no departure from, or fault against, the written records (pattola) of the swarupam, the old usage and the sayings of the sages of old, and assent is given; and provision is ordered for expenses (the day and the measure, and the storehouses and harbours to be taken in pledge for it). The muster of the forces is first ordered; services to the gods are performed; purses are laid before the Bhattatiris (learned Brahmans), he bows to them and receives their blessing. He sets nadu and city, harbour and trade in order; he has the unmarried girl married and the uninitiated boy invested with the sacred thread; he has the broken roofs of temples and groves repaired, has the kalasam (consecration) performed, endows them with funds and appoints men to them; and to Brahmans who have no means to perform their rites he gives the means. To have the nadus properly protected he appoints men in each place and provides funds.

While Mangattachan, the Elayathu, the Panikkar and the royal ministers were thus looking after the affairs of the swarupam, the Mahamakham drew near, and the Maharaja Kunnalakonatiri proceeded to Tirunavayi to hold the Mahamakham festival. Now of those who are called the 4 ministers the first is Mangattachan, who is the Ezhuttachan (writer). Next is Tinayancheri Elayathu, who was appointed desavazhi (chief of a desam, or locality) in order to install the naduvazhis (rulers of nadus). Dharmagunattu Panikkar girds on the sword of state; and in order that he might exercise the royal person and massage it,[68] [95] he was assigned princely rank and usage, and is minister of the swarupam. Lastly Para Nambi was placed in charge of the work of the royal treasury-chamber, and was given the tally-board of the vault and the purse.

5. The coming of the Portuguese and the alliance with the Kurumbiyatiri

While things stood thus, the Portuguese[69] came and made friends, built and strengthened a fort at Kozhikode and carried on trade. At that time (a foreigner from Pandi, a wearer of the round hat, a captain called Arayil Kuriyan, made war on him and) seized Kozhikode.[70] He also began to do some damage along the coast. All except Tinayancheri Elayathu had at that time gone south to war, and he had taken that opportunity to seize it. The Elayathu then wrote to the Kurumbiyatiri swarupam[71] and brought them, and (by the command of Vettakkarumakan) cut him down and drove him out (removed him), took the fort and handed it over.[72] It is said that much treasure, jewels, goods and great guns were taken that day; they say there was no end to the treasure and goods that were taken.

Because the power of Vettakkarumakan[73] was seen that day, he was from then on installed as a paradevata (tutelary deity) in this swarupam, and he took up his abode at Kozhikkavu, at Vilattikkulangara, in the kovilakam, in the Tali, at Tiruvalayanadu and at many other guard-posts. The Mother of Tiruvalayanadu and Vettakkarumakan are thus the 2 paradevatas. At that time the Kurumbiyatiri [96] was made an allied swarupam. The Kunnalakonatiri and the Kurumbiyatiri met together in the temple enclosure of Talakkulattur and settled it firmly: “Until I return from holding the Mamankam festival, you must protect the subjects so that they do not come to distress”; and they exchanged certain dignities with each other.

In the Kurumbiyatiri swarupam there are 30,000 Nayars, 6 (4) edavakas (divisions), 22 karanavar (elders), Vettakkarumakan in the fort of Palasseri, the Kurumbranadu swarupam, 32 Kuruppus, 4 Nalpadis, the Vazhunnor (ruling lords) of the 2 illams of Chembara and Nedumbara, the kings of the 2 lineages of Tuyyadu and Melkkulasseri, and Kallara, Peringuzhimuttam, Viyyur, Vengalakkal, Nelluli, Nilancheri, Attungudi, Amayamangalam, Kukkolam, Konmiyattur and the other Puliyan Nambiyars. Such is the whole sum of it.

The reason why the 4 edavakas and the rest came into being is this. Cheraman Perumal, as is known, made the 30,000 Nayars who were of divine birth the warriors of the Kurumbiyatiri and settled them in Kurumbranadu. Afterwards the men of the 64 gramams at first directed the Kurumbiyatiri to take over Polanadu. Later they decided that it should be given to the Kunnalakonatiri, and the assembly deputed 4 Nayars. They went, stated the case, and stayed there to hold the Kurumbiyatiri back; whereupon he made them his own dependants, assigned them 4 edavakas [97] and gave them the koyma. The first is the Payarmala edavaka (Paiyarmala, three katam of territory, with 500 Nayars, the Vazhunnavar of three illams, the Payyarmala swarupam, 60 taras (Nayar village units), 5 manas (Brahman houses) and 5 tanks); then the Ullur edavaka, then the Nidiyanadu edavaka, then the Puzhavayi edavaka.

When these did not come back, four-times-eight, 32, men were deputed. They too went and stayed to hold the Kurumbiyatiri back; and to each of them too he gave a rank and property (making them the holders of 32 taravadus). When they also did not come back, the assembly ordered 1,200 men to go (and work destruction) and die. These too the Kurumbiyatiri made his dependants, and granted them: “Remain as the ruling Nayars in 1,200 taras.” Considering, “What is to be done now?”, the assembly of the Brahmans deputed the Kizhiniyar (Kuzhiniyar) of the Prabhakara faction.[74] They too went and stayed, and stopped his ceremonial bathing; whereupon he kept them there, ordering: “In all the thirty-six katam no other sangham is wanted; you must take charge and be the protection here.” That is why they say there is no rival sangham in Kurumbranadu.[75]

The reason why Vettakkarumakan became their paradevata is this. After Punturakkon had subdued Polanadu, while he was making war to subdue Kurumbanadu, the Kurumbiyatiri, judging that he was no match for Nediyirippu, [98] went to Taliparambu[76] and stayed there worshipping the Lord. Then the Lord appeared to the Kurumbiyatiri and sent him away with this vision: “The king must go now without any delay. One will come from here who is able to stand and withstand Nediyirippu. If you put him in front and go on, he will stop the enemy and guard and protect nadu and swarupam.” He came back here, succeeded to the seniority and was installed (made the circuit); and when he was about to measure out the rice,[77] the god came as a warrior, held out his lap-cloth and received the rice, took Karakore Nayar by the hand and had him speak the threefold word. Know that this is the Vettakkarumakan who dwells in the fort of Palacheri, who guards and protects nadu and swarupam, removes the afflictions and great sicknesses that come upon the people, cherishes the sangham and gives food to wayfarers.

The Puzhavayi edavaka has three katam of territory, 3,000 Nayars, 10 Ammons in the Matilancheri swarupam, seven Muttol (elders) in 42 illams, Chattimangalattappan, Muvvantikkali and the Arayil Bhagavati, the Kartas who rule the 2 illams of Iranjon and Velluvasseri, and the Kartas of the 2 lineages of Tekkidam and Vadakkidam (and the Amma's rule in Puntura and the Adiparatti house). Because the funds that ought to come from this Puzhavayi to the Bhagavati of Chalayil for lamp and expenses did not come, the government, declaring that “lamp and expenses have come to a stop,” sent men. [99] When these claimed overlordship in the Puzhavayi edavaka (and a quarrel arose), laid an interdict and did damage, the people went and drove them back and cut them down. Then the Unittiri of Erumattadam and a number of warriors grew arrogant and banded together, saying, “We must subdue the country and have the honour of it.” On that day the question of honour was settled in the 18 edappadu also.[78]

Afterwards the men of the northern end arrived, set fire to the thatch of the houses and burned as far as could be (seen and) heard; and from this cause arose the battle of Kandanpalattu Kandi. Then the people and the Kartas of the edavaka took counsel together, approached the Ten Thousand and joined them. They were then taken by the hand at the Veran jack-tree and seated with them; that is why they are the Three Thousand within the Ten Thousand. These 3,000 brought the people of the north side also under themselves, and the people of the east side likewise took the part of the Three Thousand and are their allies for the battlefield. That is why both parties stand and do service at the battle of Kandampalattu Kandi. Know that they are people of the Ten Thousand. (In the same way, as each nadu was taken it settled down on this side; and so the 30,000 came into being.)

The reason for the name Tamaracheri Raja is this. After the Puzhavayi edavaka and the Kurumbiyatiri had quarrelled and much destruction had followed, the Kammans of Puzhavayi, the 3,000 Nayars, the seven Muttol and the rest joined together, approached the Kutiravattattu illam, and brought the Puravazhiya Kovil, who was king in Kottayakam.[79] [100] The Nambutiris of the two illams of Tamarasseri and Idamaram gave up their desam; the king was installed with the casting of rice as Tamarasseri Raja;[80] rice was measured out to 500 Nayars, who became his warriors; and the Bhagavati of Kottayil and Karnikkarumakan were installed as the paradevatas of the nadu. They bound themselves to each other, he on this side to be the protection of Puzhavayi and they on that side to be the protection of Tamaracheri, and dignities were assigned. The duty of safeguard (changatam) was also assigned to some of the Ten Thousand and to the lord-chief of the Five Thousand, and so it remains under protection.

Next, Payyanadu is six katam of country, with 4 kuttus (the Velliyinnur kuttu, the Tacholi kuttu, the Viyyur kuttu and the Muttadi kuttu), thus 4 confederacies. The 3 Kurumbadi (and the Akambadi) and 8,000 (30,000) Nayars make up Kurumbarnadu. They now serve at the umbrella of Punturakkon; they too have answered to the roll under the Veran jack-tree and laid their shields face down in the shade. The authority over the country on this side of Turasseri, beyond the Korappuzha,[81] together with its people, was given by the Kurumbiyatiri; they are said to be a country and people that came under the Nediyirippu swarupam by gift through a woman's succession (in the female line).

6. The state of the other three swarupams

1. Now there was of old, as is known, war between Eranadu and Perimpadappu.[82] The Perimpadappu swarupam has a great many men as its warriors: 52 [101] katam, 18 Madampis (barons) and 42 ministers, of whom Balyattachan[83] is the foremost.

There were the Kovil called Paravur, the Madattinkal Kovil, the elder Kovil of Kochi and the younger Kovil of Kochi. In the days when things stood so, there was a small lime tree in the central courtyard at Kochi; whenever the limes had set and ripened, the younger lineage and their men would come in a body, pluck them and carry them off. At that time a Bhattatiri who had received the Revati pattadanam[84] came there and asked for a feast and provisions such as are given at the Revati pattadanam. The elder lineage named the dishes and provisions, and mentioned what goes into the lime curry. “Let these limes ripen and give them to me,” said the Bhattatiri. “When the limes ripen, the younger lineage and their men will come in a body and pluck them and carry them off.” “There is a remedy for that,” said the Bhattatiri; “if a man of the Tamutiri is posted here, you will get the limes ripe.” “Then you must leave a man posted here when you go,” said the elder lineage.

So he posted his own servant there. “If I am cut down and killed, what redress will there be?” the man asked. “If you are cut down and killed, I will make the Tamutiri tread on the tiles of the fort of Kochi,” said the Bhattatiri; and he posted him and departed. Then the limes ripened. When the younger lineage and their men came and began to pluck the limes, he said, “Do not pluck the limes.” Without heeding that [102] they began to pluck. Then he laid on them an oath by the sacred feet of our Tamburan;[85] without heeding the oath they plucked the limes. Then he cut off the hand of the man who plucked, and they killed him in turn.

On hearing of it the Bhattatiri went to Kochi, took 3 tiles, came to his own illam, wrapped them in fine silk, went to the Tamutiri's kovilakam and laid them as a presentation-offering before our Tamburan. “What is this?” said the Tamburan. “Brahmans must speak the truth and may not speak untruth. The Tamutiri's man has been cut down and killed in the fort of Kochi by the younger lineage of Kochi and their men together. For that, these are the tiles of the fort of Kochi; you have only to lift your sacred foot and tread on them,” submitted the Bhattatiri. Our Tamburan's eyes grew red and his body broke into sweat. He proceeded to Tiruvilchirakkal, wrote royal letters summoning the 30,000, the 10,000 and the people of Payyanadu, provided for the men's expenses, had the Achan and the Elayathu pack shot and powder, marched straight against the fort of Kochi, battered the fort to pieces and came back. So we have heard those of former days tell it.[86]

2. In the south, when he had fought and conquered the Venadu Adikal,[87] taken tribute and joined him to himself, the Adikal, as an offering and so that this should never be altered, gave a flag for the Mahamakham and sent it; hence the tradition that this flag is called the Venadu flag. He then also gave the koyma within the temple enclosure of Chenganniyur; in that office [103] the Tirumanacheri Nambutirippadu acts as his agent to this day.

One of the paid soldiers of the Venadu Adikal stopped a Brahman at the Kannotti ferry, hindering his bath and his water-rites, and kept him detained. The Tamburan who then held the third rank (third place) came there, as it fell out, cut the man down and killed him, had the Brahman's bath and water-rites completed, and went on his way. At this the Venadu Adikal took offence, called out his forces and decreed: “South of Chettuvayi the overlordship of our Tamburan shall not run.” Then our Tamburan's anger rose; he gathered his full force, crossed at Chettuvayi, crossed the Kanjur river, went by way of Veppi and crossed the Kochi bar, set out through Kochi, passed through Chiranganadu Karappuram, crossed the Payattukkadu bridge, set out for Alappuzha, went along the Trikkunnattu river and passed Karttikappalli;[88] and as he was approaching the Udayanadu shore the Venadu Adikal came and sought refuge at our Lord's feet. He set apart, to be sent north, the money that our Lord had spent, and made over great guns and, to wipe out the offence, an elephant as well.[89]

On that day, for his conquest of the quarters, he had the hero's drum beaten, mounted on the neck of an elephant and proceeded northward, and assigned to the Lord of Tiruvanantapuram[90] the lands on which the vayittaram (vaichitryam; uttaram) was tied; so it is told of the Maharaja Kunnalakonatiri.[91] In the Kollam year 802,[92] on Wednesday the 30th of the month of Kumbham, [104] he tied on the royal crown-fillet at the Trikkavil kovilakam and ruled the land, and 4,000 lords did him service.

3. Afterwards, when he gathered an army to conquer the Kolattiri,[93] even the Kolattiri, lord of 352,000,[94] did not venture to set himself face to face against our Tamburan. At that time, with the men of the Perinchellur gramam put forward, the Maharaja had the Kolattiri's koyma within the temple enclosure of Taliparambu assigned over.[95] There it continues to this day as belonging to the Panniyur faction. The Kunnalakonatiri did homage to Verumtrikkolappan as the Lord of Taliparambu,[96] maintained the god's share and assigned dignities.

The Kolattiri Tamburan cherished the thirty-five paradevatas in the fort of Valabhattam,[97] brought the 350,000 Nayars, to the full count of heads, under one rod, and through them marked off the several classes, ordaining rank and custom alike for those attached to the inside and those attached to the outside. In the southern division he ordained, as is known, the first place in the stewardship (karisham) for Murikkancheri and the after-place in the stewardship for Mandayodan. Of the 4 illams, Chenguni and Murikkancheri are of the inside, and of these Chenguni has the after-place; Chomadavan and Mandayodan are of the outside, and of these Chomadavan has the after-place. For these four illams together he ordained one custom, namely the stewardship in the southern division, with the first place and the hand for Chenguni and Murikkancheri and the after-place and the command for Chomadavan.[98] [105]

To administer punishment and protection in the fort of Madayi[99] he granted the stewardship in the northern division, and in it to Chenicheri the mouth and the hand, the first place, the command and the right, with one custom shared with the Mavila illam also. To stop the enemy coming from the south he appointed Iraya Varman, at the Kunnivaka kovilakam, as the Tekkilamkuru Tamburan (junior prince of the south) and gave him three katam of country. In the fort of Vijayakollam, near the Kanjirottu bar, he appointed Kela Varman as the Vadakkilamkuru Tamburan (junior prince of the north) and gave him Kudayanadu and the five paradevatas. When these two had taken up the guard of the two frontiers, he appointed Rama Varman, at the Karuvallur kovilakam, as the Nalamkur Tamburan (prince of the fourth rank) and seated him near himself.

The Tamburatti (princess) who came down from the top of Ezhimala[100] he settled in the Ezhotta kovilakam and gave her property set apart for her, and he himself proceeded to the Karippattu kovilakam. After that he appointed a Jonakan to bring the 18 islands into subjection, tied a fillet of rank on him in the island, named him King of the Islands, and sent him off with orders to subdue the 18 islands and to pay in 18,000 panam every year at the fort of Valabhattam.[101] So did the Kolattiri Tamburan named Udaya Varman.

The Kolattiri Tamburan saw and desired a royal lady of the Nediyirippu swarupam, carried her off by night without anyone knowing, and kept her as his wife. Reflecting that “even if I should think of sending that lady back [106] there, the Nediyirippu Tamburans will not consent,” he granted, as the portion of her children, Nileswaram, three katam of country, and 3,000 Nayars. That is the royal line of Nileswaram.[102] To this day the kings of Nileswaram and the kings of Nediyirippu observe pollution for one another at deaths and births.[103]

7. The remaining state of Kerala told in brief

In Cheraman's country there are 17 nadus and 18 kings. Kolattiri, Venadu, Perimpadappu and Eranadu are the four swarupams. The Bauddhas having come and exerted their strength and valour, it was ordained, in order that the land of sacred rites should not decay, that there should be prosperity in the Trippa swarupam[104] on the Venadu side, sacrifices and other rites in Perimpadappu, the worship of the sword in Nediyirippu, and in the Kola swarupam the service of the Perumals who ruled in former times; if this is done as ordained, good will be seen. In Cheraman's country three kings have tied on the royal fillet, mounted the palanquin and been installed with the casting of rice. Of these the Gajapati (lord of elephants) is the Venadu Adikal, with 350,000 Nayars; the Asvapati (lord of horses) is the Kolattiri, with 350,000 Nayars; the Narapati (lord of men) is our Tamburan the Maharaja, whose retinue of 10,000 serve girt with the dagger; so it is told.[105] Among them the first place and the command belong to the Kola swarupam, and all the rest of the country are limbs of Kolam; such is the decree of Cheraman [107] Perumal.

Of the kings, eight are Samantas.[106] The Kovil kings are of five kinds, 5 lines of “Kshatriyas”: Ayalur, Sarkkara, Parappur, Padinjaredam and Madattinkizh. There are four (six) kinds of Vellalar. There are also 400 less ten lords. Their seats have their several names: edam, madam, kovilakam, kotta (fort) and kottaram (palace).

The foremost nadu is Polanadu;[107] the custom current in Polanadu is followed in the middle country (Idanadu).[108] The munnazhippadu is current everywhere; for it there are 18 customs, and where the arbitrators meet they state it in various ways. There are 4 assemblies: the war assembly (padakkuttam), the arbitration assembly (nadukkuttam), the hunting assembly (nayattukuttam) and the shade assembly (nizhalkuttam; or yogyakkuttam).[109] There is much to be told of such things as the custom of giving and taking, the custom of kanam and janmam,[110] the 4 padu, the 4 tol, the six hunts and the paradevatas of the hunt.

Between Gokarnam and Kanyakumari there are 3 temples that are, as is known, the feet, the head and the belly: the feet are Perinchellur, the head Trissivaperur, the belly Trikkalayur. Then come Tirunavayi, Triprangodu, Tiruvanantapuram, Trichamram, Tiruvilvamala, Guruvayur, Tirupanchakkalam, Alattur, Mannur, Polur, Perur, Panniyur, Paravur, Perumanam, Taliyil, Taliparambu, Kuzhiyur, Nellur, Airanikkara, Tirumannur,[111] Perumannur, Pantalur, Panniyankara, Marutur, Manniyur, Kallur, Talakkulattur, Chelangur, Trikkada, Trikkariyur, [108] Kanjirangodu, Karinkada, Kodiswaram, Uduppu, Sankara Narayanam and Gokarnam.[112]

Then the Bhadrakali shrines: Kunnattu, Kottikkunnattu, Parakkal, Mancheri, Vettattu, Kottayakattu, Kodungallur, Kurungallur, Intiyanur, Porkottacheri, Madayi, Chirakkal, Nilambara, Nileswaram, Madappalli, Putupattanam, Puttur, Kuzhalkkunnattu, Cherukunnattu, Kadalundi and Tiruvalayadu.[113] In groves such as these the Bhagavati who came down into the world in Kerala, and the Tamburattis (goddesses) and the gods, hold their sway, and so long as they do they remove the afflictions and great sicknesses that come upon the people who dwell in Kerala, and protect them.

The forms that arose from Sri Mahadeva (Siva) by one bond or another are Ayyappan, Uchamahakalan, Malan, Antimahakalan, Mundiyan, Brahmarakshasan, Karuvilli, Pottan, Bhrantan, Pullippuliyan, Karuntirukandan, Malayudavan, Dandan, Kayattan, Gulikan, Kuttichattan, (Sastavu,) Kshetrapalan and Chamundi.[114] Paradevatas such as these, and forest deities and Ganapas (attendant spirits), fill the earth and dwell in the land of Parasurama.

Thus the Maharaja Kunnalakonatiri, 10,000; the Valluvakkonatiri, 10,000; the Poralatiri king; the Kolattiri king, 350,000; the Puravazhi king in Kottayakam, 72,000; the Vettattu Mannan, 5,000; Tirumalasseri, 3,000; Perimpadappu; Ayalur, Sarkkara, Cherukkara; the Parappur king, 3,000; Padinjaridam, Madattinkizh, [109] Porotta, Nedunganadu, Tekkunkuru, Vadakkunkuru, Kakkadu, Punnattur, Ayinikkuru, Manekkulattu, Venganadu, Onenadu, Ambalappuzha, Chempakacheri, Peralottu, Muringanadu, Paiyanadu, Kottur, Irikkalikkal, the Kutiravattattu Nayar; the Eranadu Menon, 5,000; the Puzhavayi Muttukkuru Manekammal, the Pukkalayur Nambiyar, the Nayar who holds the fourth rank, the Nayar who holds the third rank, Attimannilam, Parichattu and Potta, the Parachambetta Kurichiyattu Pandala Nayar, the Kammal of Kozhikode, the Cherangadu Talachenna Nayar, the Eranadu Nayar, the Alipparambil Menon, the Tittattinkal Adiyodi, the Murikkancheri Nayar, the Penaykkotta Talachenna Nayar, the Tirumulpads of the seven-times-three and the eleven lineages of Eranattukara, Mangattachan, Tinayancheri Elayathu, the Mussad of Talayur, the Kosa of Kozhikode, and Mammali Kadavu who is the Azhi Raja (king of the sea): in this way the kings and the lesser lords between Kolam and Venadu have gone on guarding each his own royal duties.[115] To tell of the many other matters, at length and in truth, would be a very great labour.

All this has been gathered up as instruction from the Kerala-natakam told by Tunchattu Ramanujan, so that the men of the Kali age, who are of small understanding, may duly grasp it; and let the discerning take it to heart.[116]

[1] The words in round brackets here and below appear to be alternative readings or glosses, which the 1868 edition prints inside the text.

[2] Vettamudaya Kovil, “the prince who owns Vettam”, is the raja of Vettattunadu. The numbers are the fighting men each chief was reckoned to bring into the field.

[3] Nedunganadu is the country along the middle Perar (Bharatappuzha), about Pattambi and Cherpulasseri. It and several of the chiefs listed here lay well to the south of Polanadu, and probably came under the Tamutiri only in his later wars.

[4] Tamutiri (also Tamuri, Samutiri) is the title of the ruler of Kozhikode, known to Europeans, through the Portuguese, as the Zamorin. He is the same person as the Kunnalakonatiri, the Punturakkon and “the Nediyirippu” of these chapters.

[5] The words in brackets are a saying about the Ten Thousand of Polanadu: they held together like one family, serving one master for one pay. The chronicle therefore makes the Tamutiri win the country not by arms but by buying over this body and the Poralatiri's own servants.

[6] “The Manavikramans” are the Tamutiri and the princes of his house; see the note on Manichan and Vikraman in Part 2.

[7] Menokki, “overseer”, is the title of the Poralatiri's steward. The promise is kept in the last paragraph of this chapter, where he becomes the “Menon of Eranadu”.

[8] These are the four hereditary ministers of the Tamutiri: Mangattachan, the Tinayancheri Elayathu, the Dharmottu Panikkar and Para Nambi. The first is the Mangattu Menon to whom the Perumal gave his title in Part 2.

[9] The panam (fanam) is the small gold coin of Malabar.

[10] The fallen king curses the two places that betrayed him and forbids his country certain marks of wealth and honour (an upper storey, a particular bangle, a silk worn by men of rank); he then blesses the people of Kizhalur and Kurumbattur, who stayed loyal.

[11] This paragraph is a second version of the fall of the fort, set down after the first without any link: here the king is inside the fort when it is surrounded, and he slips away in disguise.

[12] Kaduttanadu (usually Kadattanadu) is the country round Vadakara, between Kozhikode and Kannur. Its rajas claimed descent from the dispossessed Poralatiri, as the chronicle says. Tamburan, “lord”, is the ordinary title of a ruling prince in Kerala.

[13] On the translation. “Kaviyadakkam” is obscure; taken as the sum of what was made over.

[14] On the translation. “Mukkalpattam” is probably the same word as mukkalvattam, a name for a temple of the lesser kind. The query in brackets is the 1868 editor's.

[15] The giving of a sword and a cloth is the form by which a ruler in Malabar invested a chief or officer with his rank.

[16] On the translation. The syntax of this sentence is loose in the original, and the part played by the Talachennor is not certain.

[17] The Tali is the Siva temple of Kozhikode, the chief temple of the Tamutiris. The meaning seems to be that authority over Polanadu was to be exercised in the name of the god, with the fines for offences going to his service.

[18] The Bhagavati of Tiruvalayanadu, whose shrine is in Kozhikode, was the family goddess of the Tamutiris; the story of the goddess carried off in a door, told above, seems meant to explain her coming.

[19] On the translation. “Nizhal” (literally “shade”) appears to be the name of this body of retainers; “nalum kolum” is obscure, apparently the daily allowance by measure.

[20] It was the custom for men who had resolved to fight to the death to dress and anoint themselves in this way.

[21] On the translation. “Oleri pachil” is obscure; some kind of running contest.

[22] The Tamutiri's house had five ranked dignities, held in order of age by its senior males (the Tamutiri himself and four princes below him); succession went through the ladies of the house, the Tamburattis.

[23] Kunninnu Konatiri, “king of the hill”, is a shortened form of Kunnalakonatiri.

[24] The fan and the whisk are insignia of royalty; to have them waved is to assume the state of an independent king.

[25] Chettis are a merchant caste of the Tamil country; the “eastern sea-coast” is the Coromandel coast of south-east India.

[26] In this chronicle “Mecca” stands loosely for Arabia and the ports of the Red Sea, the far end of the sea trade of Malabar.

[27] The Mangattachan was the hereditary chief minister of the Tamutiri, the first of his four ministers.

[28] The Marakkars were Muslim seafaring merchants of the Malabar and Tamil coasts. In the sixteenth century a family of them, the Kunjali Marakkars, commanded the Tamutiri's fleet against the Portuguese.

[29] The chronicle's two foundation stories, of the honest king and the Chetti here and of the Koya below, reflect a historical fact: Kozhikode (Calicut) grew great between the thirteenth and fifteenth centuries as an open port where foreign merchants and their goods were known to be safe. Foreign travellers of the period, among them Ibn Battuta, the Chinese Ma Huan and the Persian envoy Abdur Razzak, remark on the security of trade there.

[30] Probably the Krishna temple of Trichambaram at Taliparamba, in the Kolattiri's country in north Malabar.

[31] The Tali is the old Siva temple of Kozhikode, which still stands in the city; its uralar were the Brahman proprietors who managed it. The Tamutiris were afterwards its patrons, and the gifts to Brahmans described in the next section are presented as an atonement.

[32] The Mahamakham, in Malayalam usually Mamankam, was a great festival and fair held every twelfth year on the bank of the Bharatappuzha (the Perar) at Tirunavayi. To preside at it as “protector” was the highest mark of sovereignty among the kings of Kerala. The right belonged to the Valluvakkonatiri, the ruler of Valluvanadu, until the Tamutiri took Tirunavayi from him; the event is not securely dated, but is usually placed in or about the fourteenth century. The last Mamankam was held in the middle of the eighteenth century.

This is the origin legend of the Revati Pattattanam, the yearly assembly at the Tali temple at which the Tamutiri gave purses to Brahman scholars who had proved their learning in debate.

[38] The panam (fanam) was the small gold coin of Malabar.

[39] Here, Brahmans learned in the smritis, the books of sacred law.

At every Mamankam after the loss of Tirunavayi, a band of Valluvanadu Nayars sworn to die, the chavers, set out from her temple and tried to cut their way through the Tamutiri's guards to kill him on his platform; they were always killed.

[62] Succession in the Tamutiri's house, as in the other ruling houses of Kerala, was matrilineal and by seniority: the eldest male among the descendants of the women of the family became king, and the next eldest held the junior ranks in order.

[63] The Bhagavati of Tiruvalayanadu (Valayanadu, on the edge of Kozhikode) was the family goddess of the Tamutiris.

[66] The Kosa is the Koya, head of the Muslim merchants; the Katiyar is the Kazi (qadi), the Muslim judge of Kozhikode. Their place in the procession shows the standing of the Muslim community in the Tamutiri's state.

[69] Parangi, the chronicle's word for the Portuguese, is the Indian form of “Frank” (Arabic Faranji, Persian Firangi), the common name for western Europeans in the East. Vasco da Gama reached Kozhikode by sea in 1498. The Portuguese were soon at war with the Tamutiri and made their base with his rival at Kochi; after a peace they built a fort at Kozhikode in 1513, which the Tamutiri's forces besieged and took in 1525.

[73] Vettakkarumakan (Vettakkorumakan), “the son born of the hunt”, is a hunter god of north Kerala, held to be the son of Siva and Parvati when they took the form of forest hunters. He was the guardian deity of the Kurumbranadu kings; his chief shrine is in the fort of Palasseri (Balusseri), named in the next paragraph.

[81] The Korappuzha is the river that reaches the sea a few miles north of Kozhikode; it was the traditional boundary between north and south Malabar.

[82] Perimpadappu is the ruling house of Kochi (Cochin), named after its original home at Perumpadappu, near Ponnani. With the Tamutiri's house (here called Eranadu after its homeland), Kolattiri and Venadu, it made up the four chief swarupams among which, in the chronicle's account, Kerala was divided after the last Perumal.
